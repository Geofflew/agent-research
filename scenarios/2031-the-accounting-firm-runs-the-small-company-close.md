# By 2031 the small company's close is run by its CPA firm's agents rather than by anyone it employs or any software it picks, so growing companies put off their first audit because an audit is where the agent saving disappears, and the owner's monthly acceptance sitting becomes the one piece of finance work left inside the company

Year: 2031
Status: live
Contradicts: 2031-erp-meters-outside-agents. There the close stays with the company's ERP-native agents and the close platforms it buys; here, below the audit line, the company buys neither, because its outside firm runs one agent stack across hundreds of clients' ledgers and pays any meter as a cost of the fixed fee.
Depends on:
- C45 (the first exception-only agent closes are at unaudited companies whose books a CPA firm keeps on a fixed fee, and the lender's review report rests on the owner's monthly acceptance): this is the scenario's whole market and its one control; if firms kept billing by the hour or the independence rule did not bite, the firm would have no reason to run the close and no reason to need the owner.
- C46 (the auditor answers the client's agent with its own full-population agent, so the client's agent earns no audit saving outside frozen rules): this is why the saving stops at the audit line and growing companies delay the first audit; if auditors relied on the firm's agent, an audited company would keep the saving and the cliff would not form.
- C32 (when agents ask a person, a pre-filled answer the person approves becomes anchored inquiry, so the question should carry no proposed figure and allow "don't know"): the owner's sitting is that approval at monthly scale, which is why peer reviewers press on it and firms record it; if owners judged each decision fresh, the sitting would be ordinary oversight and nobody would need to record it.
Clocks: the AICPA's CAS benchmark survey runs every two years (2024, 2026, 2028, 2030), each reporting fixed-fee share and fees per professional; peer review runs on a three-year cycle, so a firm that moves client closes to agents in 2027 or 2028 has its review engagements inspected by 2030 or 2031; mid-market credit agreements signed in 2026 on five-year terms refinance in 2031, which is when lenders reset the revenue or loan size at which a review gives way to an audit.
Yield: 0.0

In 2031 a $20 million distributor (illustrative) has no bookkeeper, no close software contract, and no controller. Its CPA firm's agents post its entries every night in the firm's chosen ledger, the firm's accountant works its exceptions alongside about sixty other clients (illustrative), and on the fifth business day the owner spends forty minutes (illustrative) in a recorded sitting accepting or sending back each agent decision the firm lists and each covenant add-back. The lender receives a review report that names that sitting as the basis of the firm's independence. When the owner's bank asks for an audit at the 2031 refinancing, the owner pushes back, because the audit firm will run its own agent over every entry and the fee will rise rather than fall.

Mechanism: Fixed-fee firms keep the hours their agents save, so from 2027 they move unaudited clients to exception-only closes (C45). Peer reviewers in 2029 and 2030 find review engagements where no one at the client accepted the agent's work, and firms answer with a recorded monthly sitting the owner must attend, which turns the independence rule into a product feature. Meanwhile auditors of slightly larger companies run their own full-population agents and give no credit for the client's (C46), so moving from review to audit now costs the agent saving as well as the audit fee. Owners respond by staying under the audit line, and lenders at the 2031 refinancings either raise the threshold for a review or lose borrowers to lenders who do.

The owner stops paying a bookkeeper and starts attending a monthly sitting that the review report depends on. The firm's accountant stops preparing entries and works a triaged list of clients, then each client's exceptions. The lender's credit officer decides whether a recorded owner acceptance is enough to lend $15 million (illustrative) on a review. Small-company ledger vendors sell to firms, not to companies.

What would settle it: a CAS benchmark survey reporting clients per professional rising after 2026; a peer review finding citing management responsibilities on an agent-run client; a credit agreement raising the review-to-audit threshold.

Signposts: the 2028 and 2030 CAS surveys show fixed-fee share and clients per professional rising together; failing signpost, lenders at the 2031 refinancings lower the audit threshold instead, which takes the firm out of the lender's report as C45's counter-evidence warns.
