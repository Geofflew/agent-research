# By 2036 in the EU, two companies' agents agree each sale on one cleared record that both ledgers post, so the invoice stops being a document each side keeps and the payables and receivables reconciliation drops out of the close

Year: 2036
Status: retired (0002F, cap 8/year: lowest 2036 net (8.97); replaced by 2036-payment-date-is-priced-per-invoice, which keeps agents transacting with counterparties on cleared invoices but makes the payment date what they settle; shared-record mechanism and the C40 repoint review 0005 asked for moved to threads/machine-to-machine-confirmation.)
Contradicts: 2031-lender-reads-the-authority-record. There a counterparty's error sits in the cleared record until it issues a correcting document, so borrowers work a dispute queue and covenants need a cure period; here the two agents settle the sale before clearance, so by 2036 that dispute queue has nothing in it.
Depends on:
- C7 (the externally verifiable record is assembled from mandated invoice clearance and structured payment messages that outside parties already hold): the shared record is the cleared invoice itself; if clearance and structured payments do not become the outside record, each side keeps its own document and the reconciliation stays.
- C23 (the agent that reads outside documents is separated from any authority to change payee details or release payments): agents negotiating with a counterparty's agent are reading adversarial input, so the agent that agrees the record must not be the one that pays; if that separation were unnecessary, the scenario's two-agent design would collapse into one and fraud would concentrate there.
- C10 (hard limits, rejection before entry, and automatic disable sit at payment release and period lock): payment release on an agreed record is where the hard limit sits, because agreement makes the receivable and payable final at once; if journals and payments were equally reversible, there would be no reason to put the limit at release.
Clocks: EU member states may mandate domestic e-invoicing without a derogation since the VAT in the Digital Age package was adopted in March 2025, and cross-border digital reporting with structured e-invoices starts on 1 July 2030; France's and Germany's domestic mandates complete in September 2027 and January 2028; Google's agent payments protocol (September 2025) and the card networks' agent programs (April 2025) set the first rules for agents paying without a person present; the EU's review of the digital reporting rules is scheduled after the 2030 start, which places the first revision of the record format around 2033.
Yield: 0.0

In 2036 a German machinery supplier and its French customer do not exchange invoices in the old sense. The supplier's receivables agent and the customer's payables agent agree each delivery on one structured record, with quantity, price, and terms matched to the purchase order and the goods receipt, and the record is reported to both countries' tax authorities under the EU's cross-border digital reporting. Both ledgers post the same record, with the same identifier, the same amount, and the same date. There is nothing for either close to reconcile on that transaction: the receivable on one side and the payable on the other are the same cleared fact. What is left of payables and receivables work is disputes, which the agents raise against the record before it clears, and payment release, which the customer's separate payment agent performs within hard limits against the bank account registered in the record rather than any account named in a message. The three-way match, the supplier statement reconciliation, and the year-end receivables confirmation, three of the most labor-intensive parts of the 2026 close and audit, are gone for business-to-business trade inside the EU.

Mechanism: Clearance in Italy and Poland and France's platform reform already put both sides of each domestic invoice in front of the authority, Germany and Belgium already mandate structured invoices between businesses, and from 1 July 2030 cross-border invoices are reported too (C7). Agents already read invoices and emails on both sides, and payment protocols published in 2025 already define mandates for agents paying without a person present. Once both companies' agents can see the same cleared record, they stop producing and matching documents and agree the record directly, because a disagreement costs more to fix after clearance than before. Since agreeing with a counterparty means reading its agent's messages, the company keeps the agreeing agent away from payee details and payment release (C23), and the hard limit sits at release (C10). Auditors then stop sending confirmations for cleared trade and test the remaining residual: domestic trade with non-mandated counterparties, cash sales, and transactions both sides could have invented together.

The payables clerk stops matching invoices and starts working a dispute queue before clearance, deciding which price or quantity disagreements to accept. The receivables accountant stops chasing statements and starts managing collection on records that are already agreed. The external auditor stops sending receivables confirmations for cleared counterparties and moves its effort to collusion between related or newly created counterparties, because a fictitious sale that both sides clear is now the main residual risk. The tax authority holds the most complete record of the company's trade and becomes the first place a lender or auditor looks.

What would settle it: any pair of companies whose agents agree a structured invoice before clearance rather than after; an audit firm dropping confirmations for cleared counterparties; a payables product that takes the payee account only from the cleared record.

Signposts: a payables product announces agent-to-agent dispute resolution on structured e-invoices by 2029; audit methodology updates after the 2030 cross-border start reduce confirmation procedures for cleared trade; failing signpost, by 2033 companies still reconcile supplier statements to cleared invoices because the cleared record and the ledger diverge, which would mean the cleared record is not yet the shared fact.
