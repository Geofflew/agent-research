# By 2031 auditors re-prepare the client's whole routine close with their own agents instead of testing the client's agent, so each audit becomes the resolution of a few hundred disagreements between two agents

Year: 2031
Status: retired (0003F, cap 8/year with fresh-incumbent judging: lowest unprotected 2031 (12 gross, 721w, 9.60 net) when 2031-model-provider-buys-the-accounting-firm scored 13 at 625w (10.92); its core graduated into C46 and C49, so per the review 0005 graduation rule it retires when lowest at the next add. Mechanism (auditor re-prepares the routine close blind with its own agent; senior triages differences; agreed entries sampled per C49) moved to threads/auditor-reliance-on-agent-judgment.)
Contradicts: 2031-close-vendor-is-the-audited-party. Here the auditor trusts no one's agent but its own, so a vendor's attested error rate buys the client nothing.
Depends on:
- C49 (two agents agreeing is not evidence, because model errors correlate across providers): this is why the senior's afternoon goes to the agreed entries, not only the differences; if agreement counted, the 312 differences would be the whole audit.
- C51 (a usable error rate comes only from a blind sample, drawn from the items the agent marks confident, where errors that survive review sit): the auditor's agent never sees the client's answers, so its re-preparation is a blind preparation of the whole population; if anchoring did not matter, the auditor could simply review the client's output.
- C26 (every switch to auto-post or threshold change is a configuration change that restarts the evidence): clients change settings often, so controls evidence keeps restarting, while re-preparation ignores the client's configuration; if settings were stable, reliance on controls would win.
- C46 (the auditor answers the client's agent with its own agent over the whole population, so the client's agent earns no audit saving outside frozen rules): this scenario is that claim carried to the whole routine close; if auditors relied on the client's agent instead, they would test its controls and never build the comparison.
Clocks: the US amendments on technology-assisted analysis let auditors perform substantive procedures on all items in a population for fiscal years beginning on or after 15 December 2025; EY says its agents will support all end-to-end audit activities by 2028; calendar 2030 audits are fieldworked in January and February 2031.
Yield: 0.0

On a February morning in 2031 an audit senior opens the overnight run for a $900 million retailer. The firm's agent took the client's bank feeds, subledgers, contracts, and policy documents and re-prepared every reconciliation, accrual, and elimination without seeing what the client's agents booked. It then compared the two: of about 1.4 million entries, 312 differ (illustrative figures throughout this paragraph). By 10:00 the senior has sorted them into three piles: 190 where the client's agent applied a policy clause differently from the firm's reading, 96 where the firm's agent lacked a document the client had, and 26 that look like real errors. At 11:00 the senior and the client's controller work through the policy differences in one meeting, and the controller decides for each whether to change the client's agent's setting or argue the reading. After lunch the senior turns to the 1.4 million entries the two agents agreed on, which count for nothing on their own, and pulls a sample from the classes where both agents were most confident for a staff accountant to re-perform by hand, because that is where a shared error would hide.

Mechanism: Since the 2025 amendments auditors may test every item in a population, and the large firms already run agents on audit work. Testing its controls means chasing evidence that restarts every time the client changes a setting (C26). Re-preparing the routine close with the firm's own agent costs compute, not staff hours, and gives a blind comparison over the whole population (C51). By the calendar 2030 audits the firms find re-preparation cheaper than controls testing for routine classes, and audit fees for those classes fall even for clients without a clean control history, a route that does not wait for clean prior-year opinions.

The audit senior stops pulling samples and starts triaging differences between two agents. The audit manager stops reviewing sample workpapers and decides which differences are errors and which are readings of policy. The client's controller stops preparing support for sample requests and starts defending or changing the client's agent's settings against the firm's agent's reading, so the audit becomes a negotiation over policy clauses.

What would settle it: an audit firm describing full-population re-preparation of reconciliations with its own agent; routine-class audit fees falling at clients without clean prior control opinions.

Signposts: the large firms' agents reaching end-to-end audit work by 2028, as EY has said; failing signpost, by the calendar 2030 audits firms still test client controls and sample routine classes, which would favor reliance over re-preparation.
