# By 2036 companies under about $250 million of revenue no longer employ a controller, because the job has become a licensed signature that one outside professional provides for twenty or thirty companies' agents at once

Year: 2036
Status: live
Contradicts: 2036-juniors-are-hired-to-attack-the-agents. There companies rebuild in-house judgment by hiring juniors again; here the juniors were never hired, and the scarcity makes the signature worth renting.
Depends on:
- C33 (agents sign the preparer's checklist lines and each agent's named owner sub-certifies its settings and measured results rather than the numbers): the signature attaches to settings and results; if people still had to sign for the numbers item by item, one person could not sign for thirty companies.
- C30 (the mechanical first-level review goes, while the conceptual review the auditor tests stays with a manager): the controller of record is that manager, engaged by the quarter rather than employed; if the conceptual review could also go, nobody would pay for the signature.
- C17 (by about 2032 the binding constraint on adoption is the supply of mid-level reviewers): scarcity makes the signature worth renting; if mid-level reviewers were plentiful, each company would hire its own.
- C45 (a firm whose agents post a client's entries can give the lender a review report only while the owner accepts the agent's decisions, because posting for a client is a management responsibility): signing for a client's agents is a management responsibility, so the signing firm cannot be that client's auditor; if C45 is wrong, attest firms sell the signature to their own clients and the split never happens.
Clocks: the first two-year experience-route licensing cohorts finish in Texas and California in 2028 and 2029, so agent-era licensees reach a controller's usual seven or eight years of practice between 2035 and 2037; five-year loans written in 2026 refinance in 2031; peer review runs on a three-year cycle, so a supervision-ratio rule adopted in 2033 meets its first full inspection round in 2036.
Yield: 0.0

In 2036 a $120 million manufacturer with a term loan and an annual audit has a CFO, two staff accountants who handle exceptions and answer the agents' questions, and no controller. Its controller of record is a licensed accountant at an outside signing firm who holds the same signature for twenty-six companies (illustrative). Each quarter that person signs for every agent's settings, permission scope, and blind-sample error rate, and the CFO's certification and the lender's covenant rely on that signature. Between signatures the signer works one queue across all twenty-six: settings changes, exceptions above each company's dollar floor, and classes whose sampled error rate crossed tolerance. Every company has two outside firms, one that signs and one that attests.

Mechanism: Accounting firms already run many clients' closes on fixed fees (C45), and fractional controllers are an established market. As agents prepare the routine close, the signature moves from the numbers to the agent's settings and results (C33), and what remains is a conceptual review and an override decision (C30) that take hours per company per month. Because juniors were not hired, people able to make those calls are scarce by about 2032 (C17). Firms that run clients' closes offer the signature instead and give up attest work for those clients (C45). Lenders refinancing in 2031 name a controller of record in the covenant, and boards of accountancy set a ratio limit by 2033, as pharmacy boards cap the technicians one pharmacist supervises.

The CFO stops hiring a controller and starts choosing a signing firm, deciding which classes the signer covers and whether to pay for a lower ratio. The controller of record stops reviewing journals and decides across many companies which settings changes to approve, and refuses to sign on a thin blind sample. The lender's credit officer checks the named signer's deficiency record.

What would settle it: a credit agreement naming an outside controller of record; a board of accountancy discussing companies per supervising licensee.

Signposts: a 2031-vintage covenant naming an outside controller of record; a board proposal on supervision ratios; failing signpost, small companies keep an in-house controller through 2034 while juniors are still not hired.
