# By 2036 the close platform has become the company's control layer for every agent that touches money, holding the policy, the limits, and the identities used with banks, tax authorities, lenders, and other companies' agents, while the ERP has become an interchangeable posting engine

Year: 2036
Status: live
Contradicts: 2036-standard-setter-ships-the-rule-as-code. There the correct treatment is the standard-setter's reference rule set and each company records departures from it; here it is the company's own versioned policy clauses held in its control layer, which the auditor tests company by company.
Depends on:
- C10 (hard limits belong at payment release and period lock, and ERP protocol servers scope an outside agent by role and record type rather than amount or period): if ERPs add amount, period, and decision-class limits for agents themselves, the layer has nothing to hold.
- C23 (the agent that reads outside documents is kept from payee details and payment release by a deny list carved out of the human role it inherits): agents that deal with outsiders must be split from agents that hold authority across several ledgers; if ERPs ship agent principals with their own fine-grained scopes, the layer's job disappears.
- C7 (the close platform's external job becomes reconciling the ledger to records the tax authority and banks hold): if the outside record never forms, the layer stays an internal close tool.
Clocks: agent payment protocols published in 2025 (Google's in September, card networks' in April) set the first rules for agents paying on a company's behalf; EU digital reporting for cross-border supplies from 1 July 2030; SAP's extended maintenance ends in 2030 and its paid transition option at the end of 2033, the decision point at which a large estate chooses what to migrate to.
Yield: 0.0

In 2036 the system a CFO calls "finance" is not the ERP. It is a control layer that holds the company's policies as versioned clauses, each agent's posting limits and deny lists, and the credentials its agents use with the tax authority, the banks, lenders' agents, and counterparties' agents. Several ERPs sit underneath as posting engines, and replacing one no longer means re-proving the close controls. The CIO buys the ERP as infrastructure; the CFO buys the layer, priced per governed agent. The close is the layer's monthly certification that every agent stayed inside its limits and every outside record reconciles.

Mechanism: Three products already claim the job of governing agents, and only one can express a finance limit: Workday's agent registry has each agent inherit a human role, Microsoft's agent identity service can disable an agent but knows nothing about amounts or periods, and ERP protocol servers scope by role and record type (C10, C23). A limit such as "accrual journals to these accounts, under this amount, in an open period, never touching payee fields" can be written only where amounts, periods, and policy clauses are understood, which for the multi-ERP company is the close platform. As agents begin to pay and be paid on companies' behalf under open payment mandates, the agent that reads a counterparty's messages must hold no payment authority (C23), a separation easier to enforce once above several ERPs than inside each. From July 2030 reconciling every EU entity's invoices to the authority's record (C7) has to run where all ledgers are visible, which again is the layer. When SAP's extended maintenance ends that year, a company whose limits live in the layer re-proves its controls there, not in the new ERP, so the cheaper posting engine becomes a real option.

The controller stops owning a close checklist and starts owning the permission register: which agents may do what, with which counterparties, up to which amounts, and who signs for each agent's settings each quarter. The treasurer approves which outside agents the company's agents may transact with, as they once approved bank signatories. The external auditor tests the layer's limits, separations, and outside reconciliations, and reads the ERPs only through it.

What would settle it: a company replacing an ERP under its close layer without re-proving its controls.

Signposts: a close vendor holds agent identities used with a bank or tax authority by 2029; failing signpost, ERPs ship agent principals with amount, period, and payee scopes before 2030, which keeps the limits inside the ledger.
