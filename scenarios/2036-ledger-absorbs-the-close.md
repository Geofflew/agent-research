# By 2036 the month-end close no longer exists as an event or as a product category, because the ledger runs its own agents continuously and a period end is a lock on books that are already reconciled

Year: 2036
Status: retired (0002F, cap 8/year: lowest 2036 net (8.91), replaced by 2036-bank-keeps-the-small-company-ledger; continuous-ledger mechanism and its C39 dependence moved to threads/lock-day-under-continuous-close.)
Depends on:
- C39 (which write actions an ERP's protocol server gives outside agents is the ERP vendor's choice, so whether an outside agent or only the ERP's own agent can prepare the close is settled ledger by ledger): the ledger wins because its vendor keeps the write path for its own agents; if ledgers opened full write access to outside agents, a close platform could keep preparing the close on top of them. (C21, the feed-quality claim this scenario first leaned on, was merged into C26 in 0007A, and its feed-quality idea now sits in threads/review-precision-threshold.)
- C10 (for ordinary journals the kill switch is the period status the controller already holds): in a continuous ledger the period lock is the one remaining control event; if the auditor does not accept the lock as the control point, a separate close event comes back.
- C14 (agents author rules a controller approves, relied on through the route for automated controls): the ledger's rule library is where that reliance lives; if agents judge each item instead, there is no library for the ledger to own.
Note: the two-routes claim this scenario also leaned on (C2) was retired in 0007A; its idea that cross-system work stays outside the ledger now sits in threads/agent-write-scope-primitives, and this scenario is the case where it does not.

Yield: 0.0

In 2036 a finance team at a company with $500 million of revenue does not close the books, because the books are never open in the old sense. The ledger owns billing, spend, payroll feeds, and bank connections, so the subledger data that used to arrive late and dirty is native, and the ledger's own agents post, match, accrue, and amortize as transactions happen. A month end is a lock: the controller reviews a cut-off list of items the agents could not settle, approves or rejects each, and locks the period, which is the act the auditor tests. Close software as a category has gone the way of standalone consolidation tools: the vendors were bought by ledger and ERP companies or became ledgers themselves, and the portfolio view, the orchestrator, and the review pack are screens inside the ledger. What is bought is one ledger with agents included, chosen by the CFO, and priced by entity and transaction volume rather than by seat or task.

This scenario cannot be true at the same time as the one in which the close platform becomes the company's control layer over an interchangeable ERP (2036-close-layer-owns-the-ledger): here the ledger owns the data and the policy, there the ledger owns neither.

Mechanism: AI-native ledgers in 2026 already build bank matching, variance analysis, and eliminations into the ledger and report cutting fifteen-day closes to three. Because they own the subledgers, the feed-quality limit on untouched posting falls first for their customers, and their vendors decide which writes outside agents may make (C39), and their untouched share rises faster than a close platform reading another system's feeds can match. Their venture-backed customers grow into audited companies between 2031 and 2034, and their auditors test the ledger's rule library and lock events under the existing route for automated controls (C14, C10), which proves the model at audit scale. Large estates re-platforming by the 2033 deadline choose ERPs that have copied the model, because a separate close tool means proving two sets of controls at migration. Once both ends of the market run ledgers with agents inside, the standalone close platform loses its customers, and the multi-ledger company is served by the ledger vendor's own multi-entity product.

The controller stops running a close calendar and stops chasing preparers, and starts owning the rule library: they decide which rules the agents may freeze, which thresholds change, and when the period locks, and spend most of the week on the cut-off list and on policy questions the agents raise. The external auditor stops sampling reconciliations and tests the change log of the rule library and the controller's lock decisions across the year, so the audit looks more like a review of a configuration than a review of transactions. The former close-software administrator does not exist as a role; the ledger vendor's implementation partner does that work once at migration.

What would settle it: an AI-native ledger's customer passing a public-company audit with no separate close tool; a large-estate migration that retires a close platform rather than integrating it; an auditor's description of testing lock events as the period-end control.

Signposts: the first public-company audit of an AI-native ledger customer by 2033; acquisitions of close vendors by ERP vendors before the 2030 maintenance deadline; failing signpost, large migrations through 2033 keep a separate close platform, or audited ledger customers still buy one at their first audit.
