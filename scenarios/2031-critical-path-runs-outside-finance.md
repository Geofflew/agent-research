# By 2031 an orchestrator re-plans the close every hour around the people outside finance who hold its missing inputs, so business owners carry close-response deadlines in their objectives and the controller manages the company's answers rather than its accounting

Year: 2031
Status: live
Contradicts: 2031-close-vendor-is-the-audited-party. There the mid-market buys an assured close in the vendor's standard configuration and the slow step is whatever the standard leaves out; here the slow step is the company's own managers answering the agents, which no vendor configuration can buy.
Depends on:
- C32 (shipped agents wait for a finance reviewer and ask no one outside finance; when agents do ask, the question should carry no proposed figure and allow "don't know"): open questions take thought, which is what makes owners the slow step; if agents sent pre-filled estimates to approve, replies would be instant and the critical path would stay in finance.
- C33 (agents sign the preparer's checklist lines with output attached): this is why the accounting itself stops being slow; if agents could not sign lines, preparation would still wait on people inside finance.
Clocks: shipped close task managers gate rather than re-plan (a pending task cannot open until its predecessor closes, and a late one sends a delinquency email), so the first product that re-dates the plan when an input is late is a release that can be watched for; vendors' finance agent release plans run in six-month waves from 2026; the lender's thirty-day package and forty-five-day compliance certificate are the dates an orchestrator plans backward from; annual objectives set after the 2029 year-end close pay out in early 2031.
Yield: 0.0

On day two of the March 2031 quarter-end close at a $200 million distributor, the orchestrator shows 412 tasks (illustrative figures throughout): 371 signed by agents, 29 waiting on people, 12 on finance review. The path to the lender's certificate runs through 9 people outside finance: a sales director who has not confirmed which March deals carry side letters, a procurement lead who has not said whether a disputed freight bill will be paid, and a lawyer who has not given a range for a claim. Each has a close-response target in their objectives. At 16:00 the lawyer answers and the orchestrator re-dates every dependent task: the certificate still goes on day 40 if procurement answers by Thursday.

Mechanism: Today an agent that finishes early shortens nothing, because task managers only gate and the preparer chases other departments (C32). Agents sign most checklist lines (C33), so finance stops being the slow part. When a vendor ships an orchestrator that re-plans against the lender's dates, its chart shows the critical path running through owners outside finance. After the 2029 year-end close the CFO takes that chart to the chief operating officer, and response targets enter the next year's objectives.

The controller stops reviewing preparation and starts managing answers: negotiating targets with sales, procurement, and legal, and deciding which unanswered items to book as a disclosed management estimate. The sales director receives three to five open questions per quarter end, with a deadline that affects their bonus. The lender asks about estimates booked on unanswered questions.

What would settle it: a close product that re-dates its plan when a predecessor is late; a company putting close-response targets into non-finance objectives.

Signposts: by the 2028 year-end close a vendor ships re-planning against an external deadline; failing signpost, surveys after the 2029 close show delays still sit in finance review.
