# By 2031 the EU has added AI systems that prepare the financial statements of listed companies to the AI Act's high-risk list, so the first outside check on a close agent's error rate is a product conformity assessment of the vendor, not an audit of the company

Contradicts: 2031-close-vendor-is-the-audited-party. There the first outside check on an agent's error rate is a service-organization report the vendor chooses to commission and the auditor chooses to rely on; here it is a conformity assessment the law requires before the vendor may sell the agent at all.
Depends on:
- C26 (every switch of a class to auto-post or change of a threshold is a configuration change that restarts the evidence): the Act requires a new conformity assessment on substantial modification, the same rule from a product regulator; if configuration changes did not restart evidence, the two regimes would conflict.
- C25 (external auditors discount agent-prepared evidence): auditors' discounting leaves the gap product regulation fills; if auditors had relied on agent work early, regulators would have had no reason to act.
- C33 (the agent signs the preparer's checklist line and the controller checks agent-signed lines and attached output): the Act's human-oversight duty lands on that controller check; if agents did not sign lines, oversight would stay ordinary review.
Clocks: the AI Act's high-risk obligations were due from August 2026, with the Commission proposing in late 2025 to delay them to as late as December 2027; a new Annex III area such as financial reporting needs an amendment to the Act, not a delegated act, and the Commission must report by 2 August 2028 on adding new area headings; a targeted amendment adopted in 2030, as the 2025 simplification package was, with a short transition, binds from about 2031.

In 2031 a close vendor selling agents to companies listed on EU markets holds a conformity assessment for each agent that prepares figures in the financial statements. The vendor has declared each task class's accuracy on a blind sample, documented its change process, and registered the system in the EU database, and any substantial modification, including swapping the model behind the agent, triggers a new assessment. The listed company, as deployer, must keep human oversight and logs, so the controller's check of agent-signed lines is a legal duty with records a regulator can ask for. Auditors start citing the vendor's conformity documentation, so the first outside check on an agent's error rate came from product regulation. Some vendors ship a separate EU edition with slower releases.

Mechanism: The AI Act already lists high-risk uses by area, treats credit scoring of people as high-risk, and obliges the Commission to report by August 2028 on new areas. By 2028 agents prepare much of the routine close at listed companies, auditors discount their work (C25), and an EU securities or audit body raises AI in public-interest entities' reporting for that report. Because financial reporting is a new area, it needs a targeted amendment rather than a delegated act, and the Commission attaches it to a simplification or review package adopted in 2030. The Act's requirements map onto what the close already needs: declared accuracy that only a blind sample can give, a new assessment on substantial modification that matches the evidence restart on configuration change (C26), and human oversight that becomes the controller's check of agent sign-offs (C33). After a transition period the category binds around 2031.

The vendor's head of compliance stops treating model swaps as engineering decisions and schedules them around reassessments. The controller of an EU-listed company starts keeping oversight logs to a regulator's standard and decides which agent sign-offs need a second look. The external auditor reads the vendor's conformity documentation before deciding how much to test agent-prepared classes.

What would settle it: an EU securities or audit body raising AI in financial reporting for the August 2028 report; a Commission consultation on adding financial reporting systems to Annex III.

Signposts: the August 2028 report mentioning financial reporting; failing signpost, the high-risk obligations are delayed or narrowed further in the digital simplification package and no financial reporting category is proposed by 2030.
