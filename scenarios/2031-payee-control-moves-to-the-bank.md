# After a 2029 prompt-injection campaign through supplier invoices redirects payments at hundreds of companies using the same payables agent, banks by 2031 accept agent-initiated payments only to accounts they have verified against a registered payee, and the payee control moves from the ERP to the bank

Year: 2031
Status: retired (0001F, review 0004 cap: below the 2031 cap of 8; mechanism moved to threads/payee-change-verification.)
Depends on:
- C23 (the hard limit on agent write authority in payables is adversarial input, and the first agent-specific scope is a deny list on payee details and payment release out of an inherited role in which the bank-change segregation is a setting a company can switch off; C24 merged into it in 0006B): the campaign is that limit reached at scale, and the losses land where the approval was switched off, which is why the response cannot stay a company setting; if injection could be reliably prevented, or segregation were a fixed property of the role, one compromised agent would not become hundreds of losses.
- C10 (hard limits, rejection before entry, and automatic disable belong at payment release, where a ledger error stops being cheap to undo): payment release is where the new control sits, at the bank rather than in the ledger; if payments were as reversible as journals, nobody would move the control.
Clocks: EU banks have had to offer verification of payee for all credit transfers since 9 October 2025, and the UK's confirmation of payee has run since 2020 with more than 350 organisations mandated to join in 2024; US companies report business email compromise at about three in four organisations in 2025; published agent payment protocols since 2025 define mandates for payments made without a person present; companies' cyber and crime insurance renews annually, so cover terms change at the first renewal after a loss event.
Yield: 0.0

In 2031 a payables agent can still read invoices, match them, and propose a payment run, but the payment itself leaves the company only if the receiving bank confirms that the account belongs to a payee the paying company registered in advance, through its bank, by a channel no agent touches. The change followed a campaign in 2029 in which forged supplier invoices carried instructions that one widely used payables agent read as a bank change; at companies whose bank-change approval was switched off, the agent updated the vendor record and paid, and hundreds of companies lost money in the same month. Insurers then excluded agent-initiated payments to unverified payees from crime cover at the next renewals, and banks, which already ran payee verification for people, offered a registered-payee service for agent-initiated payments. In 2031 the ERP's vendor bank field is a copy of the bank's register, not the source of truth, and the payables agent's authority ends at the proposed run.

Mechanism: Today business email compromise reaches about three in four US organisations, payables agents read the same invoices and emails, and injection has no reliable prevention (C23). ERPs keep bank changes away from payers only through an approval that companies can switch off (C23). A forged invoice that one vendor's agent misreads is therefore a correlated loss across every customer with the approval off. Banks in Europe and the UK already verify payees by law for people's transfers, so extending the check to agent-initiated payments needs no new infrastructure, only a registered list. Insurers, facing one correlated loss, make verified payees a condition of cover at the next annual renewal, and companies comply because the cost is small. The hard limit C10 places at payment release moves to the bank, the one party that sees the receiving account.

The payables clerk stops verifying bank changes by phone in the ERP and starts maintaining the company's registered payee list with the bank, adding a payee only after the vendor confirms through the bank's channel. The treasurer starts deciding which payees an agent may pay without a person's release, by amount and counterparty. The controller stops reviewing vendor master changes as an ERP control, because the bank's register now decides. The external auditor tests the bank's register and the company's process for adding payees instead of the ERP's change log.

What would settle it: a bank offering registered-payee checks for payments initiated by software agents; a crime or cyber policy excluding agent-initiated payments to unverified accounts; a reported injection-driven payment loss at more than one company using the same agent.

Signposts: a reported prompt-injection payment fraud through a payables agent at any company before 2029; a bank or payment scheme publishing rules for agent-initiated payments; insurers' exclusions naming agent-initiated payments at annual renewals; failing signpost, by 2031 ERPs and payables platforms keep payee control in-house with no correlated loss reported, which would leave the deny list in the ERP as the control.
