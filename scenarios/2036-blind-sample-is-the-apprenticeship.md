# By 2036 a first-year accountant's job is preparing the company's blind sample, items the agent already did, worked without seeing its answer, and that queue is both the company's error rate and the only supervised experience that licensing boards count

Year: 2036
Status: retired (0002F, cap 8/year: lowest 2036 net (8.93); replaced by 2036-juniors-are-hired-to-attack-the-agents, a different answer to where judgment comes from; blind-preparation apprenticeship mechanism moved to threads/junior-experience-under-agents.)
Depends on:
- C12 (a usable error rate comes only from a sample in which the reviewer prepares the item blind before the two are compared): the junior's queue exists because the rate needs it; if ordinary review produced a usable rate, there would be no blind work to give a junior.
- C17 (teams shrink by not hiring juniors, and the new licensing route assumes preparation work exists for a junior to do under supervision): the scenario is the resolution of that tension, in which the blind sample becomes the preparation work; if boards stop requiring supervised experience, companies have no reason to hire juniors for the queue.
- C30 (the mechanical first-level review goes, and the conceptual review stays with a manager): the manager who settles disagreements between the junior and the agent is that surviving reviewer, and settling them is how the junior learns judgment; if the manager seat were removed too, nobody would supervise the queue.
- C28 (the blind sample sits outside the billing unit, sampled items are never billed, and refunds follow its disagreement rate): the company, not the vendor, draws and owns the sample, because the vendor's bill depends on it; if the vendor drew the sample, the junior would work for the vendor.
Clocks: Texas counts non-routine supervised work toward the new two-year experience route from 1 August 2026 and California from 1 January 2027, and the first cohorts finish in August 2028 and January 2029; audit sampling accepts a 5 percent tolerable rate when about 60 items per class show no deviations, so twenty task classes need 1,200 to 1,900 blind items a year, roughly one junior's year; the revised international audit evidence standard governs audits from mid-2029; the 2028 and 2029 licensees reach manager level between 2035 and 2037.
Yield: 0.0

At 8:00 on a Tuesday in 2036 a first-year staff accountant at a $400 million company opens a queue of seven items (illustrative, about one day's share of 1,200 to 1,900 a year) that the sampling engine drew overnight from work the agents finished last week: a legal-fee accrual, a bank reconciliation for a new account, a lease modification, and an intercompany elimination among them. They see the inputs the agent saw and nothing of the agent's answer, and they prepare each item from scratch. At 11:30 the comparison unlocks: five match, one differs by a rounding convention, and the lease modification differs in substance. At 13:00 their manager reviews the two disagreements with them, decides the agent was right on the rounding and wrong on the lease, records the agent's error against the lease class, and signs the morning's work as supervised non-routine experience. At 15:00 the auditor's agent pulls the sample log for the quarter, and once a quarter the controller sees each class's disagreement rate beside the setting it justifies and the vendor's refund it triggers. The company hires two juniors a year for this queue, not to prepare the close, which the agents do, but to measure it, and those juniors are the only people in the company who learn to prepare every kind of item.

This scenario cannot be true at the same time as the one in which the close vendor draws the sample across its customers and employs the blind preparers (2031-close-vendor-is-the-audited-party): here the company keeps the sample because the vendor's bill depends on it, and the junior's training stays inside the company.

Mechanism: Today the licensing route that replaces thirty credit hours with a second year of supervised experience assumes juniors prepare work under supervision, while agents take that preparation first and companies stop hiring juniors (C17). The only preparation left that has a purpose is the blind sample (C12), and the company must own it because refunds follow its disagreement rate (C28). Sixty items per class per year across twenty classes is about one junior's year, and the manager who settles disagreements (C30) is exactly the supervisor the boards require. After the 2028 and 2029 cohorts, boards accept the sample log as evidence of non-routine work, and by 2036 the first juniors trained this way are becoming managers themselves.

The first-year accountant stops ticking agent output and starts preparing items cold, and learns every task class through the queue. The manager stops reviewing agent work item by item and spends an hour a day settling disagreements, which is now the main teaching in the department. The controller decides how many juniors to hire by how many classes need a fresh rate. The licensing board reads sample logs as experience evidence. The auditor tests how the sample was drawn and whether the preparer was truly blind.

What would settle it: a state board or the national association saying whether blind preparation of agent-completed items counts as non-routine supervised work; a company hiring juniors explicitly for a re-performance queue; a close vendor offering a blind mode that hides the agent's answer until the preparer submits.

Signposts: a board ruling on agent-era experience after the August 2028 and January 2029 cohorts apply; entry-level postings that describe re-performance or quality sampling of agent work; failing signpost, by 2032 juniors are still not hired and samples are drawn by vendors or audit firms, which would leave the companies without a training queue.
