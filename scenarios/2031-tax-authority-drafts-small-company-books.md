# By 2031 tax authorities in invoice-clearance countries draft the books of small companies from cleared invoices, payroll filings, and bank data, so a small company's close becomes disputing the authority's draft and the bottom tier of outsourced bookkeeping disappears

Year: 2031
Status: retired (0001F, review 0004 cap: near-duplicate of 2031-lender-reads-the-authority-record (both rest on the tax authority holding both sides of each invoice); distinct mechanism moved to threads/tax-authority-record-as-evidence.)
Depends on:
- C7 (the externally verifiable record is assembled from mandated invoice clearance and structured payment messages that outside parties already hold): the authority can draft the books only because it holds both sides of each invoice; if clearance data stays incomplete or unusable, there is nothing to draft from.
- C27 (the first exception-only closes are at unaudited private companies, run by accounting firms on fixed fees across many clients): this scenario takes the bottom of that market away from the firms; if firms had already captured small companies with agent-run closes at near-zero cost, a free authority draft would add little and the firms would keep the tier.
- C32 (agents ask without a proposed figure, because a pre-filled estimate the owner approves becomes anchored inquiry): the authority's draft is the largest pre-filled estimate in finance, and the owner who validates it is anchored on it; if anchoring on a pre-filled figure were not a real risk, the disputes described below would not be needed.
- C19 (the value of a faster close is captured in the lender's package and cost per close, not in public reporting): small companies never paid for speed, only for cost, which is why a free draft wins; if speed mattered to them, a paid close would still have a reason to exist.
Clocks: Chile removed companies' obligation to keep purchase and sales books in 2017 once the authority built the registers; Italy has drafted VAT registers for about two million quarterly filers since September 2021, with validation dropping the duty to keep registers; France's domestic mandate covers small companies from 1 September 2027; EU member states must align domestic real-time reporting with the EU model by 1 January 2035, and cross-border reporting starts on 1 July 2030, so a draft built for one member state can be reused across the EU in the early 2030s.
Yield: 0.0

In 2031 a French or Italian company with €3 million of revenue no longer keeps its own books in any active sense. Each month the authority's portal shows a draft ledger built from the company's cleared sales and purchase invoices, the payroll it reported to social security, and the payment data its banks report, and an agent working for the company compares the draft to what the company knows and raises disputes: an invoice booked to the wrong period, a purchase that is personal rather than business, a supplier invoice the company rejected but the supplier cleared anyway. Once a quarter the owner validates the draft, and the validated draft is the company's books for tax, for its annual filing, and for its bank. The bookkeeping firms that served companies of this size lose the work, and the ones that survive sell dispute handling and advice rather than data entry or a close. Close software below a few million euros of revenue is the authority's portal and the disputing agent.

Mechanism: Chile and Italy already build companies' purchase and sales registers from cleared invoices and let validation replace keeping them, and Chile cited half a million inconsistent returns a year as the reason. France's mandate brings small companies onto clearance in September 2027, so the authority holds both sides of nearly all their business invoices (C7). Payroll is already reported in real time in France and Italy, and payment data reaches authorities through the e-reporting parts of the reforms. Drafting a full small-company ledger is the same step as drafting VAT registers, applied to the remaining accounts, and it pays the authority back in fewer inconsistent returns. Because small companies buy on cost rather than speed (C19), a free draft displaces the paid close, and the paid service that remains is the disputing agent.

The small company's owner stops sending receipts to a bookkeeper and starts validating a draft each quarter, deciding which disputes to raise, and is anchored on the authority's figures in exactly the way a budget owner is anchored on an agent's pre-filled estimate (C32). The bookkeeping firm's partner stops selling monthly closes to small companies, keeps the larger clients, and sells a dispute service priced per dispute won. The tax inspector stops opening audits from mismatched returns and starts reviewing the disputes companies raised against the draft, which become the authority's main audit signal. The bank stops asking small borrowers for management accounts and reads the validated draft.

What would settle it: an authority drafting accounts beyond VAT registers, such as a draft profit and loss or corporate tax return, for small companies; data on how many Italian quarterly filers validate the draft registers; a bookkeeping firm reporting loss of small clients to the authority's drafts.

Signposts: an Italian, Chilean, Portuguese, or French announcement of a pre-filled corporate income tax return or draft accounts for small companies by 2029; bookkeeping firms in Italy or France reporting falling small-client counts after September 2027; failing signpost, authorities stop at VAT drafts through 2031, which would leave the small-company close with the firms.
