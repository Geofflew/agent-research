# By 2031 a mid-market lender in an invoice-clearance country reads the borrower's sales, purchases, and cash from the tax authority and the banks, so the borrower's close stops producing the numbers and starts disputing the record, and credit agreements add a cure period for errors only a counterparty can correct

Year: 2031
Status: live
Contradicts: 2031-close-vendor-is-the-audited-party. An outsider who reads the outside record does not pay for assurance over how the borrower's close was prepared.
Depends on:
- C41 (Indian lenders already read the borrower's tax returns and bank feed directly instead of a package): a lender will take the tax record in place of the close once it can be routed under consent; if European direct lenders do not copy that, the package stays and there is no record to dispute.
- C7 (the verifiable record is assembled from mandated invoice clearance and structured payment messages): if clearance and ISO 20022 remittance do not make the record complete enough to compute revenue and cash, the lender keeps reading the borrower's ledger and the cure has nothing to cure.
- C29 (the review concentrates on the covenant add-back schedule because the slack lives in negotiated add-backs): if the slack sat in managed entries rather than add-backs, the lender could not leave the entries to the authority's record.
- C19 (the value of agent speed is captured in the lender's monthly package, not in public reporting): the lender is the one outsider on a monthly clock, so it is the first to stop waiting for the package and read the record instead; if the package's speed were what lenders valued, a faster close would keep it.
Clocks: Poland's national invoice system mandatory from 1 February and 1 April 2026; France's issuance obligation for large and mid-sized companies from 1 September 2026 and small companies from 1 September 2027; EU digital reporting for cross-border supplies from 1 July 2030; Fedwire and cross-border SWIFT payments on ISO 20022 since July and November 2025; five-year loans written in 2026 refinance in 2031.
Yield: 0.0

In 2031 a Polish or French company with €40 million of revenue and a direct-lender term loan no longer sends a monthly package. Under a consent in the credit agreement, the lender's agent pulls cleared invoices from the authority's platform and payment messages from the banks each week and computes revenue, margin, and cash conversion itself. What the lender reads is the counterparties' record, not the borrower's: a supplier's duplicate invoice or a customer's withheld credit note sits in the lender's covenant figure until that counterparty issues a correcting document, because a cleared invoice can be corrected only by another cleared document. The 2031 vintage therefore writes a record cure into the covenant: the borrower may file a list of disputed invoices with evidence, and the lender excludes them from the test for one or two quarters while the counterparty corrects them.

Mechanism: From 2026 and 2027 Poland and France route invoices through the authority, so it holds both sides of most domestic invoices, and ISO 20022 remittance lets cash be matched to them outside the company (C7). Indian banks already score small borrowers on GST and bank data (C41). Direct lenders already argue over add-backs rather than entries (C29), so the 2031 refinancing vintage asks for the record. The first covenant tests on authority data then fail on counterparty errors the borrower cannot fix in its own ledger, and lenders, unwilling to call defaults on a supplier's mistake, accept the cure mechanism.

The borrower's controller stops writing flux commentary and works a weekly dispute queue, deciding before each test date which disputes to file for cure. The accounting firm that ran the close sells dispute filing per lender relationship. The lender's portfolio manager decides cure requests on the evidence and watches borrowers whose cure lists grow, because a long list is either a fraud signal or a supplier in trouble. A large customer's agent starts rejecting doubtful invoices at clearance rather than after.

What would settle it: a credit agreement in a clearance country granting the lender direct access to the borrower's authority invoice data; a covenant clause excluding disputed cleared invoices from a test.

Signposts: a French or Polish direct lender announces monitoring from e-invoicing data within a year of the September 2027 small-company deadline; failing signpost, tax-secrecy or data-protection rulings block delegated lender access, or the 2031 vintage still asks for the monthly package.
