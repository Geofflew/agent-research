# By 2036 companies that stopped hiring juniors without keeping a training queue have nobody left who can judge the agents, and a wave of material weaknesses for inadequate accounting resources pushes boards to buy judgment from outside at any price

Year: 2036
Status: retired (0002F, cap 8/year: lowest 2036 net (9.30); replaced by 2036-standard-setter-ships-the-rule-as-code; the unmanaged-shortage mechanism (material weaknesses for inadequate resources, KPMG fiscal 2025 data) moved to threads/junior-experience-under-agents.)
Contradicts: 2036-juniors-are-hired-to-attack-the-agents. There companies hire juniors again to attack the agents and grow their own reviewers; here they did not, and the gap is the result.
Depends on:
- C17 (teams shrink by not hiring juniors, and by about 2032 the binding constraint is the supply of mid-level reviewers able to judge exceptions): the scenario is that constraint left unmanaged; if companies kept hiring juniors or mid-level reviewers stayed plentiful, there would be no gap to fail on.
- C30 (the mechanical review goes and the conceptual review the auditor tests stays with a manager): the manager seat survives on paper, so the failure shows up as a competent-person deficiency in a control the auditor still tests; if the conceptual review could also be removed, there would be no seat to find empty.
- C33 (each agent gets a named owner who sub-certifies its settings and measured results; C35 merged into it in 0006B): the people signing for agents in 2034 and 2035 are the ones the auditors find unable to explain them; if signatures attached to the numbers rather than to settings, the gap would show differently.
Clocks: material weaknesses citing inadequate accounting resources rose 14 percent in fiscal 2025 against 2024, and KPMG ties the rise to fewer graduates and retiring CPAs; the 2026 graduating class is the 2032 to 2036 mid-level, so the shortfall from juniors not hired in 2026 to 2028 reaches manager seats from about 2033; material weaknesses are disclosed in each annual report, and the 2034 and 2035 10-Ks are the first in which the managers who were never juniors on agent-era teams hold review seats.
Yield: 0.0

In 2036 an audit committee chair at a $2 billion company reads the third consecutive annual report in which the auditor found a material weakness in the review of agent-prepared estimates, and the reason given each time is that the reviewer could not explain how the agent reached its figure or recompute it. The company had stopped hiring juniors in 2026, moved most preparation to agents, kept the manager review the auditor tests, and filled the manager seats in 2033 and 2034 with the most senior people it had left. Those people had spent their careers reviewing agent output and had never prepared the items themselves. Across the market, disclosures citing inadequate accounting resources, already rising in 2025, have tripled (illustrative), concentrated in exactly the classes agents prepare. Boards respond by buying judgment wherever it exists: hiring back retirees on contract, paying audit firms' former managers well above any 2026 salary band, and paying accounting firms to re-prepare samples. The profession's scarcest asset is now someone who prepared a reserve by hand before 2026, and the companies that kept juniors on a blind-sample queue are recruiting targets for everyone else.

Mechanism: In fiscal 2025 material weaknesses for inadequate accounting resources were already rising with the graduate shortfall. Agents take preparation first and companies stop hiring juniors (C17). Most companies do not run a blind sample at scale because it costs about a junior's year per twenty classes, so no one prepares items anymore. The manager review survives because the auditor tests it (C30), and the people signing for the agents (C33) are promoted into it from a cohort that only ever reviewed agent output. Skills that are not used decay, as the ironies-of-automation literature found in process control. From about 2033 the auditor's walkthroughs find reviewers who cannot re-perform what they approve, and the 2034 and 2035 annual reports disclose it.

The audit committee chair stops accepting the agent's error rate as comfort and starts asking who in the company could prepare each class by hand. The CFO stops treating junior hiring as a cost to cut and starts competing for the few people who prepared items before agents, at prices that erase the savings. The external auditor starts testing reviewer competence directly, asking reviewers to recompute a sampled item in the walkthrough. The retired controller of 2026 is back on contract at three companies.

What would settle it: material weakness disclosures after 2030 that cite reviewers' inability to evaluate agent-prepared work; auditor walkthrough practice that asks reviewers to recompute agent items; salary data showing pay for people with pre-agent preparation experience rising faster than other finance roles.

Signposts: material weaknesses for inadequate accounting resources rising again in the fiscal 2027 to 2030 annual reports as juniors go unhired; an inspection finding on reviewer competence over automated preparation; failing signpost, entry-level finance hiring recovers by 2030 because companies hire juniors for sampling, which would make the apprenticeship scenario the more likely one.
