# By 2031 a private equity sponsor buys one agent close for all its portfolio companies from a single outsourced finance provider, so a portfolio company's finance team shrinks to the people who answer the agents, and lenders must decide whether a close the sponsor procures is still the borrower's own

Year: 2031
Status: live
Contradicts: 2031-model-provider-buys-the-accounting-firm. There the investors who own accounting firms' advisory halves sell them to a model provider at exit; here private equity keeps the close capacity, because one provider closing forty portfolio companies' books on one calendar is worth more to a sponsor than the sale price, and the person accepting each company's agent decisions sits at the sponsor.
Depends on:
- C45 (a firm whose agents post a client's entries can give the lender a review only while the client's owner accepts the agent's decisions, because posting is a management responsibility): here the owner who accepts is the sponsor's portfolio finance lead, so the review firm must be one the sponsor does not control; if posting did not end independence, the provider could also review, and lenders would see one firm doing everything.
- C33 (agents sign the preparer's checklist lines and each agent's named owner sub-certifies its settings and results): the named owners of forty companies' agents sit in one sponsor team, which is what lets one team run forty closes; if people signed for numbers item by item, the work could not be pooled.
- C19 (the value of agent speed is captured in the lender's monthly package): the sponsor buys the shared close to deliver forty lender packages on one calendar; if lenders read only annual statements, there would be no monthly deadline to pool.

In 2031 a sponsor with forty (illustrative) portfolio companies has one close contract. Its provider's agents post every company's entries, and the sponsor's portfolio finance lead accepts each company's agent decisions in a weekly queue sorted by company. Each company keeps a CFO and one or two people who answer the agents' questions. At the 2031 refinancings, lenders add a clause that the borrower's statements must be reviewed by a firm neither the sponsor nor the provider controls, because the person accepting the books works for the owner rather than the company.

Mechanism: Sponsors already put portfolio companies on one outsourced finance provider and charge them for shared services. As agents take the preparation, the provider runs forty closes on one platform and the agents' named owners pool in the sponsor's team (C33). The sponsor gains forty lender packages on one calendar (C19). The independence rule then forces a split (C45): the provider posts, the sponsor accepts, and a third firm reviews, which lenders write into the 2031 refinancings.

The portfolio company CFO stops managing a finance team and answers for one company inside the sponsor's queue. The sponsor's portfolio finance lead accepts decisions for forty companies. The lender's credit officer asks who accepted the books and who reviewed them.

What would settle it: a sponsor contracting one close provider across its portfolio; a credit agreement naming the reviewer's independence from the sponsor.

Signposts: by 2029 a large sponsor announces a portfolio-wide close contract; failing signpost, by the 2031 refinancings portfolio companies still each run their own close.
