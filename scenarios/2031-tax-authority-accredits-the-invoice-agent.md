# By 2031 the French tax authority, not an auditor, insurer, or AI regulator, is the first body that certifies a finance agent, because its accreditation of e-invoicing platforms is extended to the agents that issue, accept, and reject invoices on a company's behalf

Year: 2031
Status: retired (0003F, cap 8/year under the review 0007 fresh-incumbent rule: lowest unprotected 2031 on fresh judgement (13 gross, 750w, 10.50 net) when 2031-critical-path-runs-outside-finance scored 13 at 588w (11.04); review 0006 had asked to keep it as a new-certifier world, so its full text stays here and its mechanism (French platform accreditation renewals in 2028-2029 adding agent conditions; C7, C23, C50) is in threads/french-platform-invoice-statuses.)
Contradicts: 2031-eu-makes-close-agents-high-risk. There the first outside check on a close agent is a product conformity assessment under the AI Act, which reaches listed companies' reporting; here it arrives two years earlier through tax law for every company that invoices, and it tests what the agent may do to the tax record, not its error rate.
Depends on:
- C7 (the verifiable record is assembled from mandated invoice clearance and structured payment messages that outside parties hold): the tax authority certifies the agent because the agent now writes the record the authority relies on; if the outside record never formed, the authority would have no stake in who writes it.
- C23 (the hard limit on agent authority is adversarial input): an agent that accepts or rejects supplier invoices reads documents from outside the company, so the accreditation requires that the agent that reads an invoice cannot also change the supplier's bank details or release payment; if injection were not the binding risk, the authority would certify only message format.
- C50 (in France an issued invoice cannot be modified, only corrected by a new invoice or credit note through the platform, so issuing joins payment release and period lock as a point where an agent's error is costly): an agent's wrong invoice lands in the authority's record at once, which gives the authority its stake in who may issue; if issued invoices could be edited before lock, the authority would have nothing to certify.
Clocks: France requires every company to be able to receive electronic invoices through an accredited platform from 1 September 2026, and large and mid-sized companies to issue them from that date, with small companies from 1 September 2027; the tax authority publishes the list of accredited platforms and can withdraw accreditation; accreditation is granted for three years and renewed, so platforms accredited in 2025 and 2026 renew in 2028 and 2029, the first point at which new conditions can be imposed; EU cross-border digital reporting starts on 1 July 2030.
Yield: 0.0

In 2031 a French platform's accreditation file includes a section it did not have in 2026: the agents it lets customers run. For each agent the platform declares whether it can issue an invoice, accept or reject a received invoice, or change a supplier's payment details, and the accreditation allows the first two only when the agent that reads incoming invoices is separate from any agent that touches payment details, and only when each status change it sends to the authority's system carries the identity of the agent and of the person who set its authority. A mid-sized manufacturer's controller learns the agent's limits from the platform's accreditation terms, not from the auditor.

Mechanism: From September 2026 every invoice a French company sends or receives passes through an accredited platform, and agents start to accept and reject supplier invoices there, which changes the status of the invoice in the authority's record (C7). The first disputed rejections and fraudulent acceptances made by agents show up in the authority's data during 2027. At the 2028 and 2029 accreditation renewals the authority adds agent conditions, because it is the only party that both holds the record and already licenses the software that writes to it. The conditions follow the separation C23 describes, and issuing is gated because an issued invoice cannot be edited (C50). By 2031 every French company that uses an agent on invoices uses one the authority has, in effect, certified.

The controller stops designing agent permissions on invoices and chooses from the configurations the platform's accreditation allows. The platform's compliance officer files agent declarations with each renewal and answers the authority's queries on disputed rejections. The auditor reads the accreditation as the control over who changes invoice status. The tax inspector, auditing a company, asks which agent accepted an invoice and who set its authority.

What would settle it: an accredited platform's terms or the authority's accreditation specification mentioning automated or agent actions on invoice status; an authority statement on fraudulent acceptance by automated systems.

Signposts: by the 2028 renewals the French authority publishes conditions for automated status changes; failing signpost, by 2030 accreditation still covers only message format and security, leaving agent authority to companies.
