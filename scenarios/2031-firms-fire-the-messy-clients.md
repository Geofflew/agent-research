# By 2031 accounting firms running agent closes on fixed fees price each client by its exception rate, so clients with messy subledgers are repriced or dropped and a new segment of companies too disorderly for an agent close appears

Year: 2031
Status: retired (0001F, review 0004 cap: lowest net in 2031 under the cap of 8; mechanism moved to threads/cas-multi-client-queue.)
Depends on:
- C27 (the first exception-only closes are at unaudited private companies, run by accounting firms on fixed fees across many clients): the firm keeps the saving only when its staff review exceptions alone, so exceptions are its cost; if firms billed hourly, a messy client would simply pay more and nobody would be dropped.
- C21 (the untouched share rises by task class, limited by the quality of the subledger feed rather than by the model): the exception rate is set by the client's feeds, not by the agent, which is why the firm prices the client and not the software; if the model were the limit, every client would improve together and none would stand out.
- C29 (in the lender-driven close the review concentrates on the covenant add-back schedule): the add-back review is a fixed monthly cost per client that the firm cannot automate, so it sets the floor of the price; if add-backs were agent-proposed, the floor would fall and more messy clients would stay profitable.
- C34 (the firm whose agents post a client's entries cannot also give the lender the review report unless the client's owner oversees the work): the dropped clients must find both a new operator and someone to oversee or review, which raises their cost twice; if one firm could do both, being dropped would cost less.
Clocks: firms moved from 53 percent billing by the hour in 2018 to 10 percent by 2024, so fixed fees are already the norm when agent closes spread; fixed-fee engagement letters renew annually, so repricing happens at the first renewal after a firm measures exception rates per client; the 2031 refinancing vintage of loans written in 2026 is when dropped clients' lenders ask who now prepares the package.
Yield: 0.0

At 8:00 on business day three of the March 2031 close, an accountant at a 200-person outsourced accounting firm opens one queue for sixty clients. Fifty-one show no open exceptions: their agents matched bank feeds, posted accruals, and attached output to every checklist line. Nine show between four and forty exceptions each, and two of them, a construction subcontractor with job costs kept in spreadsheets and a distributor whose inventory system exports a file by hand, account for three-quarters of the morning. By noon the accountant has cleared the two, at about ten times the minutes per client of the other fifty-eight, and logged the time against each. Once a quarter the firm's partners read exceptions per client-month beside each client's fixed fee, and at renewal the two outliers receive a choice: fix the feed, pay a price set by their exception rate, or find another firm. Across the industry the clean clients' fees fall, the messy clients' fees rise or the firms drop them, and the dropped companies form a segment of businesses too disorderly for an agent close that hire in-house bookkeepers again or buy premium service from firms that specialize in them.

Mechanism: Firms already bill most clients on fixed fees and run many clients' closes from shared queues (C27). Agents clear clean feeds and leave exceptions where feeds are poor (C21), so the firm's cost per client becomes almost entirely its exception rate plus the fixed add-back review (C29). Firms that log time against exceptions see a long tail of clients that cost several times their fee, and the annual renewal is the moment to act. Repricing or dropping is cheaper than fixing a client's systems, and the dropped client cannot have the same firm prepare and review (C34), so its cost rises twice. The segment that results is defined by data quality rather than size or industry.

The firm's accountant stops treating clients as equal units and works the queue by exception load, logging minutes against each. The firm's partner decides at renewal which clients to reprice and which to drop. The messy client's owner decides whether to fix the feed, pay more, or bring bookkeeping back in-house. The lender asks, at the 2031 refinancing, who now prepares the package and how clean the feed is.

What would settle it: a firm's own data on exceptions per client-month under agent closes; engagement letters priced by exception volume or feed quality; firms publicly declining clients whose systems cannot feed an agent close.

Signposts: a CAS benchmark survey reporting pricing by data quality or exception volume by 2029; an outsourced firm announcing minimum feed standards for new clients; failing signpost, firms keep flat fixed fees through 2031 and absorb messy clients, which would mean exception cost is small next to the add-back and advisory work.
