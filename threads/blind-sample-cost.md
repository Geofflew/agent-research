# Who prepares the blind sample once the preparer is gone, what does it cost, and must it estimate dollars rather than counts?

Status: live
Opened: session 0007C
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C12, C17, C3, C26; threads/junior-experience-under-agents, threads/who-anchors-who-discounts
Sources: [sampl-pcaob-as2315-audit-sampling], [sampl-minitab-attribute-sample-59], [sampl-practools-nauditmus], [sampl-rule-of-three-wikipedia], [cpaexp-tsbpa-pathway-b-faq]

What we know so far: The audit sampling standard lets an auditor conclude a control works when 60 sampled items show no deviations against a 5 percent tolerable rate, and allowing one defect raises the sample from 59 to 93 for the same confidence, so a per-class blind sample is small if the agent is clean and grows quickly if it is not. A count of deviations says how often the agent is wrong, but tolerable misstatement is set in dollars, and monetary unit sampling selects in proportion to amount and takes every high-value item, so a sample designed to tell the auditor something about misstatement would weight large items rather than draw uniformly. Thesis C17 now says the junior's queue becomes the blind sample, which answers who prepares it: the trainee whose qualifying experience must be supervised work.

What it would take to become a claim: a cost figure (hours per blind-prepared item by class), and either an auditor statement that a company's blind sample is usable as evidence or a vendor shipping blind preparation.

Open sub-questions:
- With 20 task classes, 60 to 93 items each is 1,200 to 1,900 blind-prepared items a year; is that one junior's year, and does it fit the two-year licensing experience?
- Should the sample be drawn by count (deviation rate, the controller's question) or by dollar (misstatement, the auditor's question), and can one sample serve both?
- If the agent improves and the sample shows zero deviations for two years, does the sample shrink, and who decides?
