# What does the client's controller do during audit weeks once the auditor tests whole populations with its own agent?

Status: live
Opened: session 0008C
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C25, C11, C12; scenarios/2031-auditor-agent-re-prepares-the-close; threads/who-anchors-who-discounts
Sources: [expq-kognitos-pbc-list], [expq-fieldguide-ai-enhanced-audits]

What we know so far: Today the client's audit weeks are retrieval work done by the same tired close team. A vendor guide to PBC lists says "Almost all of the client-side work is retrieval and tie-out rather than judgment", that the list arrives 30 to 60 days before fieldwork, that documents are often owned by another function, and that "stalled PBC responses are the most common reason audit timelines slip" [expq-kognitos-pbc-list] (vendor). An audit-software vendor says full-population analytics work when client data sits in consistent cloud formats and that auditors "must validate data completeness and accuracy across the full dataset" [expq-fieldguide-ai-enhanced-audits] (vendor). So full-population testing moves the client's job from pulling sample support to proving the population is complete; neither source says the request list shrinks. The 2031 scenario's controller, who spends a meeting defending policy readings against the auditor's agent, has no present-day analog in these sources.

What it would take to become a claim: a survey of client hours spent on audit requests before and after an auditor moves a class to full-population testing, or an audit firm's client guide saying which requests full-population procedures replace.

Open sub-questions:
- Does the client's request list under full-population testing become one data-completeness request per system plus a list of differences to explain?
- Who at the client answers a difference that is a policy reading: the controller, or the owner of the agent's setting?
- Does the controller start the audit with a reconciliation of population counts (entries in ledger = entries given to auditor) as the first deliverable?
