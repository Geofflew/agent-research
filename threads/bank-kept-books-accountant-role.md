# What is left for the owner and the outside accountant when the bank keeps the small company's books?

Status: live
Opened: session 0008C
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C41, C27, C45; scenarios/2036-bank-keeps-the-small-company-ledger; threads/lender-reads-tax-filings
Sources: [expq4-accountingweb-starling-accounting-portal]

What we know so far: A UK bank already sells the ledger and is opening it to accountants for year-end only. Starling, which "acquired ember in august 2025", says that "if the client is only using starling accounting, they would need to perform any actions themselves", positions accountants to "step in at year-end to ensure compliance", and plans a portal where partners can post journals and import opening balances [expq4-accountingweb-starling-accounting-portal] (secondary). So the 2036 scenario's first step is shipped: the owner categorises in the bank, and the accountant's job shrinks to year-end adjustments; the scenario's turn, the accountant reviewing the bank's books for the bank's credit team, would put C45's independence question on the bank's side.

What it would take to become a claim: a bank using its own bookkeeping product's data in small-business credit decisions, or an accountant engagement scoped as review of bank-kept books.

Open sub-questions:
- Who owns a correction the accountant makes in the bank's ledger: the client, the bank, or the accountant?
- Does the bank's credit team see the categorised books or only the transactions?
