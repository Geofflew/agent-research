# Will an insurer backing a vendor's accuracy warranty be the first outside party to accept an agent's error rate?

Status: live
Opened: session 0006B (from retired C15 and C16, merged into C18)
Yield: 4.81
Touches: C28, C10, C12, C14, C23, C40, C25 (C18 retired 0006A); scenarios/2031-crime-insurer-licenses-the-payables-agent
Sources: [ins-munichre-aisure-faq], [ins-munichre-aisure-page], [ins-armilla-lloyds-ft], [ins-itoo-aisure-moonstone], [ins-insurers-exclude-ai-beinsure], [ins-cpa-firms-ai-underwriting-dig-in]

What we know so far: AI performance cover (Munich Re aiSure, Armilla via Lloyd's, iTOO) pays on measured drift past an agreed threshold, not on a single mistake, and carriers are preparing AI exclusions for correlated loss. Retired C15 held the insurer would accept a rate before any auditor; retired C16 held insurers would impose aggregate limits and automatic disable. C18 now carries the attestation route and the correlated-loss limits; the insurer-first ordering is unproven.

Retired C18 (0006A) held that the per-class rate reaches outsiders through a SOC 1 Type 2-style examination of the vendor, with the customer's blind sample as a complementary user entity control, rather than through an insurer's warranty or the AICPA and AIUC attack-testing offering, and that the same report would have to test aggregate limits and automatic disable. ISA 402 lets a user auditor rely on a Type 2 report and GS 007 says a Type 1 report cannot reduce substantive procedures; the AICPA and AIUC offering pairs a SOC 2 opinion with live attack testing, launching early 2027.

What it would take to become a claim again: any insurer writing cover on a finance or close agent (not a chatbot), or a close vendor contract with an insured accuracy warranty.

Open sub-questions:
- Does any AI policy name operational controls (limits, kill switch, human approval) as conditions?
- Is a wrong rule across a class insurable at all, or excluded outright?
- Does any close or ERP vendor's SOC 1 report scope an AI or matching component (trust-center descriptions)?

0002F (futurist): a second insurer route, through crime cover rather than AI performance cover. Social engineering endorsements make cover conditional on "an Employee verified the Transfer Instruction according to a pre-arranged callback" and on documenting it [insfar-intact-callback-endorsement]; excess SEF products sit over primary sublimits of a minimum $250K and up to $1MM, with call-back verification "in force before making changes to key contact information" [insfar-intact-excess-sef-faq]; brokers now ask for higher limits on what was "a small sublimit" [insfar-trisura-sef-limits-ibc]. ISO CG 40 47 01 26 (generative AI exclusion, CGL) became available January 2026 and Berkley applies an absolute AI exclusion in D&O, E&O, fiduciary, none yet on crime [insfar-iso-genai-exclusion-ibc]. AIUC-1 is certified by an accredited auditor, valid one year [insfar-aiuc1-standard]. Scenario 2031-crime-insurer-licenses-the-payables-agent (11.29 net, kept) argues the word "Employee" in the callback clause keeps agents off payee changes before any auditor asks, and the rewritten clause is the first outside rule admitting them. Counter: the callback clause names a person, no crime form found that mentions agents, and difference-in-condition excess cover can buy around the callback.
What would make it a claim (B): a crime or SEF form or application that mentions AI agents or automated verification; a claim decision where an automated system did the verification.

0002F draft scenario (not added, 8.32 net, below lowest 2036 8.97): "By 2036 lenders to private mid-sized companies accept financial statement insurance in place of an audit or review, so the insurer hires the examiner, the premium prices the close agent's measured error rate, and the lender reads the premium and the limit instead of an opinion." Builds on Ronen's 2002 financial statement insurance proposal (never sold because misstatement could not be priced) plus AI performance cover since 2025; Contradicts 2036-verifiability-is-priced-in-the-spread. Judge: insurer-appointed examiner vs C14/C25/C34 independence, 2031 insurer step asserted. What would lift it: any insurer or MGA writing misstatement cover for private companies, or a credit agreement accepting a policy in place of reviewed statements.

Grounder (0002F) on 2031-crime-insurer-licenses-the-payables-agent, the highest-scoring live scenario. It stands on three claims: C23 (if error rather than manipulation were the binding risk, the crime insurer has nothing distinct to price and the scenario collapses into the vendor-attested route); C40 (if no outside name or register check existed, the insurer has no substitute for the employee and excludes agents outright, the failing signpost); C25 (if auditors relied on agent work early, the audit route sets agent authority first). The step no claim defends is the premise itself: that crime cover's verification condition already makes payee changes by an agent uninsured. Draft claim for B (premises now in sources): "Social engineering fraud cover pays only if an employee verified the transfer instruction by callback and documented it [insfar-intact-callback-endorsement], and excess carriers require call-back verification before changing key contact information [insfar-intact-excess-sef-faq], so a payables agent that changes or pays to a changed vendor account leaves that loss uninsured; the first outside rule that keeps agents off payee changes is an insurance condition, which reaches mid-market companies at their next annual renewal, before any audit or ERP rule." Nearest claims: C23 (says payee details are carved out because of injection risk, not why companies comply) and C40 (when the carve-out lifts). Counter: the form names an Employee but does not mention agents; difference-in-condition excess can buy around the callback; no claim decision on an agent-verified change found.
