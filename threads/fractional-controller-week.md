# What does an outside controller's week look like when they serve many companies' closes?

Status: live
Opened: session 0008C
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C27, C34, C30, C17; scenarios/2036-controller-of-record; threads/cas-multi-client-queue; threads/cas-firm-independence
Sources: [expq-meridian-one-accountant-1m-book], [expq-mom-project-fractional-controller-posting], [expq-creative-planning-outsourced-controller-posting]

What we know so far: Today's fractional controller is close to one client at a time, and the many-client ratio exists only for bookkeepers. A real-estate lender's posting asks for a fractional controller at "20hrs per week" who prepares the monthly, quarterly, and year-end closings and leads the auditor relationship [expq-mom-project-fractional-controller-posting] (primary, posting). An outsourced-controller role at a wealth firm asks for the "Ability to work with multiple clients" without a count [expq-creative-planning-outsourced-controller-posting]. A vendor says a bookkeeper running manual closes carries 15 to 25 monthly clients before the close slips, and that with an agent close "Review time scales with exceptions, not with transaction volume" [expq-meridian-one-accountant-1m-book] (vendor). The 2036 scenario's twenty-six companies per signer therefore needs the bookkeeper's ratio to reach the controller's seat, which only happens if the controller's hours become exception and settings review.

What it would take to become a claim: a non-vendor figure for clients per outside controller or hours per client per month, or a firm describing a signer who sub-certifies agent settings for several clients.

Open sub-questions:
- Is the outside controller's hour per client spent on exceptions, on settings changes, or on the owner's sign-off sitting (C34)?
- At what ratio do peer reviewers or lenders start asking how one person can sign for this many companies?
