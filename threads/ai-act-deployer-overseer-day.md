# Who is the named overseer of a close agent under the EU AI Act, and what is their day?

Status: live
Opened: session 0008C
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C33, C12, C38; scenarios/2031-eu-makes-close-agents-high-risk; threads/subcertification-agent-owner
Sources: [expq3-eu-ai-act-art26-deployer-obligations], [expq3-eu-ai-act-art14-human-oversight]

What we know so far: If close agents were listed as high-risk, the deployer's duties already name a person and a day. Deployers "shall assign human oversight to natural persons who have the necessary competence, training and authority", "shall monitor the operation", keep logs "at least six months", and on a risk or serious incident "inform the provider" and "suspend the use of that system" (Art 26) [expq3-eu-ai-act-art26-deployer-obligations] (standard). The overseer must be able "to remain aware of the possible tendency of automatically relying or over-relying on the output", "disregard, override or reverse the output", and "interrupt the system through a 'stop' button" (Art 14) [expq3-eu-ai-act-art14-human-oversight] (standard). That maps onto C33's named agent owner: the owner becomes a legally assigned overseer with a duty to know about anchoring, which is C12's blind sample written into law, and the stop button is C10's auto-disable.

What it would take to become a claim: a Commission or national guidance applying Art 26 to an accounting or finance deployment, or the Art 112 review naming financial reporting.

Open sub-questions:
- Does "competence, training and authority" exclude the junior staff accountant who signs today's sub-certification (C33)?
- Does suspending use on a serious incident mean stopping the close mid-period, and who posts the remaining entries?
