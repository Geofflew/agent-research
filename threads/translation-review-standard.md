# Does the translation profession's full-review standard predict what happens to the close preparer seat?

Status: dormant
Opened: session 0007C (from C9's former title, removed in the 0007C rebuild)
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C9, C17, C30, C12
Sources: [mtpe-dgt-in-figures], [mtpe-iso-18587-scope], [mtpe-routefifty-gov-machine-translation], [mtpe2-cbc-translation-bureau-cut], [mtpe2-frey-llanos-paredes-voxeu], [mtpe2-cnn-translators-losing-work], [mtpe2-microsoft-support-raw-mt]

What we know so far: The European Commission's machine translation went from 19 million pages in 2017 to 890 million in 2025 while ISO 18587:2017 required full human post-editing of every machine output; the seat was renamed reviewer rather than removed, yet Canada's Translation Bureau plans to cut 339 of about 1,350 positions through attrition and many freelance translators reported lower income. Microsoft publishes raw machine translation for support pages people act on. Reviewer 0002 judged the analogy thin for the close: translation volume grew because translation became cheap, while close volume is fixed by transactions, and the full-review standard's weakness (the reviewer reads the output before the source) is now argued in C12 from finance and medical evidence.

What it would take to become a claim again: evidence that a review-every-item rule for agent-prepared close work is being proposed by a standard-setter, or close data showing headcount falling through attrition rather than layoffs.

Open sub-questions:
- Did outsourced per-task accounting providers (per-reconciliation pricing) lose volume before in-house teams shrank, as translation freelancers did?
