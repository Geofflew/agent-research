# What does adversarial testing of finance agents look like as a job today, and could juniors do it?

Status: live
Opened: session 0008C
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C23, C17, C12; scenarios/2036-juniors-are-hired-to-attack-the-agents; threads/junior-experience-under-agents
Sources: [expq4-accel-adversarial-ai-engineer-posting]

What we know so far: Today AI red teaming is a senior security job, not an accounting one. A posting asks the hire to "lead adversarial testing by designing, scoping, and executing red team assessments across our ai ecosystem" and wants "6+ years of direct, hands-on experience testing the security of ai/ml systems" [expq4-accel-adversarial-ai-engineer-posting] (primary, posting). So the 2036 scenario needs the work to split: security staff attack the model and its tools, while someone who knows how books are fooled (fake vendors, cut-off games, add-back relabelling) writes the finance attacks. Nothing found yet shows finance staff doing the second part.

What it would take to become a claim: an audit firm or insurer requirement for adversarial testing of a finance agent, or a finance team job posting for agent testing.

Open sub-questions:
- Would a licensing board count designing finance attack cases as "non-routine accounting work" (Texas pathway)?
- Is the C12 blind sample the cheaper substitute for red teaming, or a different test?
