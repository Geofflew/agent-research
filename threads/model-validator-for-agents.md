# Who validates a close agent's model the way a bank validates a vendor model, and does any rule cover agents?

Status: live
Opened: session 0008C
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C38, C42, C12, C25; scenarios/2036-auditor-and-client-must-use-different-models; threads/model-retirement-control-change
Sources: [expq3-fed-sr-26-2-revised-model-risk-guidance]

What we know so far: The banking model-risk regime explicitly leaves agents out. The Federal Reserve's revised interagency guidance (SR 26-2, 17 April 2026) keeps effective challenge "performed by individuals with the appropriate expertise", says "the validation of vendor products, either by internal or outside parties" is important though banks "may not receive from the vendor the underlying code, data, or methodology", names "benchmarking to other models", and then says "generative ai and agentic ai models are novel and rapidly evolving. as such, they are not within the scope of this guidance" [expq3-fed-sr-26-2-revised-model-risk-guidance] (primary). So the 2036 different-models scenario cannot borrow a bank rule for dissimilar challengers; the validator's day for a close agent today has no regulatory template, and benchmarking against another model is permitted practice, not a requirement.

What it would take to become a claim: an auditing or banking regulator bringing agentic AI into model-risk scope, or an audit firm describing benchmarking a client's agent against a different model.

Open sub-questions:
- Does the exclusion leave bank finance teams using close agents with no model-risk review at all, or does the bank's own policy reach them?
- Would the auditor's agent (2031-auditor-agent-re-prepares) count as "benchmarking to other models"?
