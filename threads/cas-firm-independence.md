What we know so far: AICPA nonattest rules (ET 1.295.040, quoted in JofA) let a firm keep an attest client's books only if the client assumes management responsibilities and designates a person with suitable skill, knowledge, and/or experience to oversee and accept the results; the overseer need not be able to re-perform the work. A review requires independence; a compilation can be issued with a 'we are not independent' paragraph. The code has no AI-specific rule (JofA Aug 2026). In C27's design the owner reviews nothing, so the firm likely cannot be reviewer of record for the lender.

Draft claim (0006B, scored 7 net < median 8; g1 n2 m1 s1 c2 t1 e1 r2; not kept):

> ## C34. The CPA firm that runs an unaudited client's close with agents, as C27 predicts, cannot also give the lender the review report unless the client's owner actually oversees and accepts the agent's work, because independence rules forbid the firm from assuming management responsibilities and a review requires independence, so the owner's monthly approval of the agent's exceptions and adjustments becomes the safeguard on which the lender's report depends.
> 
> The chain has three steps. First, the AICPA code lets a firm keep an attest client's books only if the client assumes all management responsibilities and oversees the service by designating an individual with suitable skill, knowledge, and/or experience who evaluates the results and accepts responsibility for them; the JofA's example is a review client whose staff identify each expense and the CPA only books it. Second, a review report cannot be issued by an accountant who lacks independence under any circumstances, while a compilation can be, provided the report states that the accountant is not independent. Third, in C27's design the firm's agent prepares every entry and the owner receives the package and reviews nothing, so no one on the client's side has evaluated the results, and the firm has in effect made the entries itself.
> 
> This changes C27's conclusion that the firm becomes both buyer and reviewer: it can be the reviewer of the close or the reviewer of record for the lender, but not both unless the owner takes the overseer's seat. The code makes that seat easy to fill, since the overseer is not required to possess the expertise to perform or re-perform the services, which means the safeguard is an owner approving the agent's output, the anchoring C12 describes placed in the person least able to resist it. Consequence: each month the owner, not the firm's accountant, approves the agent's exception decisions and the covenant add-backs of C29 on a screen that records the approval as the independence safeguard; firms that cannot get that approval split the work, sending the review to a second firm, or deliver a compilation saying they are not independent. Signpost: lender or peer-review findings on review engagements for CAS clients citing management responsibilities, within the first peer-review cycle after a firm adopts agent-prepared closes.

Draft claim (0008B, not kept):

> ## C52. The accounting firm that keeps an attest client's books with agents can stay independent only while the agent's rules are the client's choices and the ledger is the client's own, because the AICPA's information-system rules treat designing a financial information system, or running it as an ongoing outsourced function, as a management responsibility that no client overseer can cure, so the owner's monthly acceptance that C45 relies on saves the review only for the bookkeeping, not for an agent the firm configures and maintains.
> 
> The code draws two lines that an agent crosses. Under the information-system services interpretation, threats cannot be reduced to an acceptable level if the member designs or develops a financial information system for an attest client, defined as one that aggregates source data underlying the financial statements, and designing means determining how it will function, process data, and produce results; services in which the client outsources to the member an ongoing function or process, such as systems monitoring, support, or maintenance, are a management responsibility even if an individual with suitable skill, knowledge, and experience from client management is overseeing them. Configuring purchased software is allowed only as long as the client makes all decisions on the options. Under the hosting interpretation, effective since July 2019, a firm impairs independence if it is the sole host of the client's system or the custodian of data the client cannot reach without it, while a client that gives a CPA permission to access its own books to perform bookkeeping is not hosting. The AICPA's 2026 guidance adds that developing or hosting an AI system for an attest client can raise independence concerns and that the existing rules still apply.
> 
> This narrows C45: the owner's monthly sitting is the safeguard for the entries, but the agent's matching rules, thresholds, and posting logic are the system, and a firm that writes and maintains them continuously is designing and running it. Consequence: the firm's agent runs as a user in a ledger the client licenses; the owner, not the firm's accountant, picks the agent's thresholds and rules from options the firm presents, and signs each change; the firm keeps no copy of the books the client lacks; and a firm that wants to tune the agent across clients itself issues compilations rather than reviews to those clients' lenders. Signpost: an AICPA ethics committee question, peer review finding, or state board ruling on firm-configured agents for attest clients within the current three-year peer review cycle confirms; a ruling that agent configuration is bookkeeping counts against.
