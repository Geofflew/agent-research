What we know so far: The callback to a number on file is the documented control against payment-redirection fraud, and the FBI still recommends hanging up and calling an independently found number. But the person in the loop is now spoofable: an Arup finance worker sent about $25.6 million across 15 transactions after a video call in which the CFO and colleagues were deepfakes, and the call cleared his own doubts. FinCEN reports rising use of deepfake media to get past identity checks at banks. A machine check against the receiving bank's own record now exists: from 9 October 2025 EU payment providers must offer Verification of Payee on credit transfers, returning match, close match, or no match against the name the bank holds, and the UK has run Confirmation of Payee since 2020 under a regulator mandate. Neither check proves the account belongs to the real vendor; the EPC says VoP cannot be relied on to identify a person, and a fraudster's account opened in the vendor's name passes.

What it would take to become a claim: evidence that AP or ERP platforms call VoP or CoP at vendor-master change (not only at payment), or a measured fraud reduction attributed to CoP or VoP.

Open sub-questions:
- Can a close or AP platform request a VoP check when a bank detail changes, before any payment is initiated?
- Does VoP's optional ID-code field (VAT, LEI) close the vendor-name gap, tying the check to the tax-authority record of C7?

Draft claim (0008B, not kept):

> ## C44. Whether a company can keep an outside agent away from vendor bank details is decided by the ledger's object model rather than by the deny list C23 expects the company to write, because Business Central keeps vendor bank accounts as a separate record behind an approval workflow and outside its standard vendor API, while Xero keeps the bank account number inside the contact that its protocol server's create and update tools write under one contacts scope, so on ledgers built like Xero an agent allowed to maintain vendors can also change where they are paid.
> 
> The two designs are documented. Xero's API defines BankAccountDetails as the bank account number of a contact, its server offers create-contact and update-contact, and the granular scopes its own server requests for new apps split invoices, payments, bank transactions, and manual journals but keep contacts as one scope. QuickBooks Online likewise carries the vendor's payment bank account as a field group inside the vendor. Business Central's standard vendor API has no bank fields, vendor bank accounts sit on their own card, Microsoft marks their fields as sensitive and ships a Vendor Bank Account Approval Workflow, and its own Payables Agent creates new vendors blocked, leaving bank accounts to human callbacks. C39 says which writes a ledger exposes is the vendor's choice; this adds that the granularity of those writes is fixed by the data model the ledger already had.
> 
> This disagrees with Xero's granular scope design, which separates transaction types but not the one field payment fraud targets. Consequence: on Xero or QuickBooks the bookkeeper either keeps vendor setup and every contact edit as manual work, which blocks the agent from onboarding new suppliers, or accepts an agent that can redirect payments; on Business Central the agent creates the vendor, the AP clerk approves the bank account, and the deny list is a configuration the company never writes. Signpost: Xero or Intuit adding a separate scope or approval for contact bank details by the end of 2027 counts against; a reported redirection through an agent's contact update on such a ledger counts for.
