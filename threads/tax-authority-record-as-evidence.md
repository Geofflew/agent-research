# Does anyone use the tax authority's invoice record as close or audit evidence?

Status: live
Opened: session 0007C
Yield: 10.55 (0008C C7 +0.78; 0008B C50 add +9)
Graduated: C50 (0008B, French e-invoice issuance as irreversible point)
Touches: C7, C27; threads/lender-view-of-agent-statements
Sources: [ctc2-gst-gstr2b-auto-drafted-itc], [italy-sdi-prefilled-vat], [ctc2-sii-chile-prefilled-f29-from-dte], [vida-digital-reporting], [eu-einvoicing-2026-mandates]

What we know so far: India builds a read-only purchase-credit statement (GSTR-2B) from suppliers' filings that the buyer cannot edit and is told to reconcile to its own books. Italy drafts VAT registers from its clearance system, and Chile built the purchase and sales register and removed companies' obligation to keep their own. EU digital reporting for cross-border business starts 1 July 2030. Thesis C7 (sharpened 0007C) says the payables and receivables preparer will start each close from the agent's list of differences between the ledger and the authority's register, and the auditor will test that residual list. No source yet shows an auditor or lender actually doing this.

What it would take to become stronger: a Big Four or local-firm practice note in Italy, India, or Chile using authority data as revenue or purchase evidence; a close or reconciliation product that ingests GSTR-2B or SdI registers; the typical mismatch rate between books and GSTR-2B.

Open sub-questions:
- How large is the residual in practice (share of invoices mismatched each month)?
- Does the auditor treat the authority's register as external evidence under the audit evidence standard, given the authority built it from the company's own suppliers' filings?
- Chile found 512,000 inconsistent returns in 2016; is authority data clean enough to anchor a close?
