# What is left for the accountant on the last day when the books are reconciled during the month?

Status: live
Opened: session 0008C
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C10, C19, C33; scenarios/2036-ledger-absorbs-the-close; threads/what-replaces-the-checklist
Sources: [expq-beancount-forum-continuous-close-8-to-2-5-days], [expq-rillet-continuous-close-vendor-guide]

What we know so far: Where daily recording and weekly reconciliation already run, the close shrinks to a few hours of review, variance analysis, approval, and distribution, but it does not disappear. A first-hand forum account reports "Month-end workflow (4 hours):" of final verification, variance analysis, and reports, with the close moving from 8 days to 2.5; a CPA replying says "Continuous accounting doesn't eliminate the need for period-end close procedures" and that formal review, approval, and certification are "still needed, just faster" [expq-beancount-forum-continuous-close-8-to-2-5-days] (primary, anecdote, small books). A vendor guide cites APQC-derived figures of 5 days or fewer for top performers and a median of about 6, and calls the period end "Close as confirmation" [expq-rillet-continuous-close-vendor-guide] (vendor). So the 2036 scenario's lock is plausible as an event of hours, and what survives is the signature, the variance explanation, and cutoff, which is where C10 puts the lock-day work.

What it would take to become a claim: a named mid-market company's account of the lock day (who signs, how long, what is reversed), or primary APQC data on what tasks remain after continuous reconciliation.

Open sub-questions:
- Who writes the variance explanation when the agent has posted all month: the agent, with the controller approving, or the controller?
- Does cutoff testing at lock become the main control once reconciliations are continuous?
