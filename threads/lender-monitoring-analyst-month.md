# What does a private-credit monitoring analyst do each month with borrowers' packages, and what would a verifiable close change?

What we know so far: One direct lender's postings describe the monitoring month as reading packages and keying them. The analyst will "Review monthly financial statements, quarterly covenant compliance certificates, and annual audited financial statements" and "Monitor liquidity, cash flow, and borrowing base availability"; the intern posting assigns "monthly financial statement reviews for an assigned portfolio of 20-25 companies" and splits time "Financial Monitoring & Analysis (40%)" and "Portfolio Administration & Data Management (30%)" [expq2-starmountain-portfolio-management-analyst-posting], [expq2-starmountain-portfolio-monitoring-intern-posting] (primary, postings, same firm). So about a third of the analyst's month is moving numbers from packages into the lender's systems; a borrower whose close produces a standard verifiable feed removes that third first, before any change in the spread the 2036 scenario prices.

What it would take to become a claim: a lender or fund administrator stating hours per borrower per month, or a lender pricing or terms sheet that rewards a direct data feed.

Open sub-questions:
- With 20-25 borrowers per analyst, which package arrives late most often, and does a late package trigger a covenant default notice or a phone call?
- Does the analyst recompute the add-back schedule (C29) or accept the certificate?
