What we know so far: The SEC proposed optional semiannual reporting on 5 May 2026 (Federal Register 7 May, comments closed 6 July 2026); Form 10-S keeps the 40- and 45-day deadlines, its statements must be reviewed but not audited, and the SEC admits it has no estimate of the incremental cost of the quarterly auditor review while inferring a saving of about 12% of the annual audit. Nasdaq's 2019 survey put average quarterly compliance cost at $334,698 (median $75,000). King & Spalding notes some debt covenants require quarterly reporting regardless and companies would still need quarterly internal processes. Typical private-credit covenants want monthly financials in about 30 days and quarterly statements plus a compliance certificate in about 45. Thesis C19 (0007C) now rests on the Federal Register text.

What it would take to become stronger: a count of how many public companies have covenants requiring quarterly reporting; the adoption date of the semiannual option; any company electing semiannual reporting and saying what happened to its close cadence.

Open sub-questions:
- If a company elects semiannual reporting, does its close cadence fall, or does the lender's calendar keep it monthly?
- Does the quarterly auditor review continue voluntarily (SEC question 25), and does that keep a quarterly close alive?
