# Which product ends up governing a company's finance agents: the ERP, the identity provider, or the close platform?

Status: live
Opened: session 0001F
Yield: 0.72
Touches: C2, C24, C23, C7, C10; scenarios/2036-close-layer-owns-the-ledger, scenarios/2036-ledger-absorbs-the-close
Sources: [agentperm-workday-asor], [agentperm-ms-entra-disable-agents], [ap2-docs-mandates], [erpmcp-workday-mcp-external-agent], [agentperm-netsuite-ai-connector-permissions]

What we know so far: three products already claim the job of governing agents. Workday's agent registry gives each agent its own identity, but the agent "inherits your policies, rules, security groups, and permissions" of a human role [agentperm-workday-asor]. Microsoft's agent identity service can disable an agent object or block token issuance by conditional access, but revocation stops tokens and does not scope or unwind finance transactions [agentperm-ms-entra-disable-agents]. Workday's protocol endpoint for external agents lists a step to configure security policies for agent skills and no amount or threshold scoping [erpmcp-workday-mcp-external-agent]. None of the three expresses a limit in amounts, periods, accounts, or decision class, which is the shape C2, C10, and C24 say finance needs. Google's agent payments protocol defines open mandates that carry constraints for an agent paying without a person present [ap2-docs-mandates], so the counterparty side of the problem has a published shape while the company side does not. No source yet shows business-to-business payables or receivables agents transacting with each other.

What it would take to become a claim: a shipped product (close platform, ERP, or identity provider) that scopes an agent by amount, period, or account across more than one ERP; or a documented case of a company writing one agent limit that applies across several ledgers. A counter-finding would be an ERP shipping agent principals with amount, period, and payee scopes, which keeps the limits inside the ledger and supports the ledger-absorbs scenario instead.

Open sub-questions:
- Does any close platform today hold agent permissions spanning several ERPs, or only read from them?
- Does Workday's or SAP's agent registry add amount or period scopes on its roadmap, and with what date?
- Are any B2B payables or receivables agents transacting with counterparties' agents under AP2 or a card-network program, and who issues the mandate on the company side?

Draft claim (0008B, not kept):

> ## C54. Regulation is giving outside parties read access to a company's financial record through the tax authority's copy rather than through the company's ledger, so the first outside agents that act on a company's numbers will read the authority's record and write nowhere, which leaves the ERP vendor in control of write access, as C39 holds, while ending its control over who can read.
> 
> Every mandate found points the same way. India's central bank made the GST network an information provider so that lenders receive the borrower's filed returns on consent, and C41 shows banks now underwriting on them. Poland's tax administration publishes every pair of tax number and bank account daily for bulk checks in accounting systems. The EU Data Act obliges a cloud service to offer open interfaces free of charge, but to facilitate switching, for portability and interoperability, and to export exportable data on the customer's request, while stating that providers need not develop new technologies or services. Against this, the one law that forced write access, the payment rule that makes banks accept payment orders from outside initiation services without a contract, covers bank accounts, not ledgers; and the ERP vendors present their own gateways as the governed point: Sage describes its AI gateway as letting AI models query and use Intacct data while maintaining governance and control.
> 
> This disagrees with that gateway design as the place outside agents will connect for the numbers that matter to outsiders: a lender, a payee check, or a tax agent reading the authority's copy needs no gateway and no permission from the vendor. It ties C39 to C41 and C7: write stays vendor-controlled and unregulated, read moves to the public record. Consequence: the controller's question about an outside agent shifts from which ERP role it gets to which consented authority feed it reads, and since that copy holds what was filed rather than what was posted, the accountant reconciles the ledger to the filing each month before an outsider does. Signpost: a regulator mandating third-party write or action access to a cloud ledger before ViDA's 2030 start counts against; another consent rail exposing tax filings to lenders, as India's does, confirms.
