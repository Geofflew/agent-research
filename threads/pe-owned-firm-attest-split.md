# How do private-equity-owned accounting firms split attest from agent-run bookkeeping, and who checks the line each day?

Status: live
Opened: session 0008C
Yield: 0.0
Touches: C34, C27; scenarios/2031-ownership-split-after-platform-failure; threads/cas-firm-independence
Sources: [expq3-jofa-peec-aps-private-equity-discussion-memo]

What we know so far: The profession already separates the two entities, and the line runs through network-firm rules. The AICPA ethics committee's private-equity task force says "only licensed cpa firms can provide attest services", with "clearly defined firewalls" for investors in the nonattest entity; "the nonattest entity would be considered a network firm of the attest firm", while the investor and its other portfolio companies "would generally not be considered network firms", and network firms "are subject to independence requirements for financial statement audit and review clients" [expq3-jofa-peec-aps-private-equity-discussion-memo] (secondary, JofA on PEEC). So C34's independence limit binds the sister nonattest company too: the agent-run bookkeeping arm cannot post for a client whose review the attest arm signs unless the owner oversees. The 2031 scenario's failure needs this line to be crossed, not absent.

What it would take to become a claim: the ET 1.220 alternative practice structure text, or a PEEC adopted interpretation from the 2026 meetings on network firms in PE structures.

Open sub-questions:
- Who at the attest firm checks, engagement by engagement, that the sister company's agent did not execute the client's transactions?
- Does peer review test this line (three-year cycle)?
