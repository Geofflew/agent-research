# Will balance confirmations become system-to-system, with no person on either side?

Sources: [conf-as2310-confirmation-standard], [conf-thomsonreuters-confirmation-home], [conf-thomsonreuters-confirmation-bank-apis], [conf-trintech-intercompany-kpis-ebook], [conf-visa-intelligent-commerce-launch]

What we know so far: PCAOB AS 2310, effective for fiscal years ending on or after June 15, 2025, keeps the auditor in control of sending and receiving, allows an electronic intermediary if the company cannot override it, and for cash and receivables allows the auditor to obtain evidence by directly accessing information maintained by a knowledgeable external source (para .24). Confirmation.com (vendor) reports 4,300+ banks and departments, bank-side AutoProcess APIs returning machine-readable receipts, and an 80% time saving against about 10 minutes per manual response. Intercompany matching is already automatic inside one vendor's ledger (a Trintech customer claims 90%+ of 100,000+ records). Agent-to-agent payment protocols (AP2, Visa Intelligent Commerce) cover consumer purchasing, not confirmation. No primary evidence of agent-to-agent balance confirmation yet.

What it would take to become a claim: something C7 does not already say. C7 holds that the close's external job is reconciling to records banks and tax authorities hold; a claim here needs the auditor side: evidence that auditors use para .24 direct access to bank systems at scale, or that the company's agent reconciliation and the auditor's direct access read the same bank feed, changing what the controller prepares for the audit (e.g. no cash confirmation package). AS 2310 via r.jina.ai mirror is flaky (captcha on first fetch); find a stable host (SEC approval order on govinfo) before citing.

Open sub-questions:
- What share of bank confirmations are answered by API today?
- Does direct access by the auditor make the company's cash reconciliation redundant evidence?

0003F grounder (iter200, payment-date scenario; 5 earlypay-* sources): C2FO's early-payment pricing is per invoice in the arithmetic (an APR applied to each invoice by days paid early) [earlypay-c2fo-apr-vs-discount-per-invoice-rate], but the rate is set by the supplier, by a C2FO-recommended rate, or by the buyer's market for fixed and variable divisions [earlypay-c2fo-pricing-strategy-express-accept], offers are evaluated in a daily batch at market processing [earlypay-c2fo-building-an-offer-market-processing], and supplier automation chooses which invoices and when, not the rate [earlypay-c2fo-automation-invoice-rules-schedule]. EU instant payments: euro-area receiving from 9 January 2025 and sending from 9 October 2025, non-euro states 2027, charges no higher than ordinary transfers [earlypay-ecb-instant-payments-regulation-deadlines].
Finding: the per-invoice price exists today, set by people and one platform's algorithm in a daily batch; no source shows agents on both sides setting it. That makes 2036-payment-date-is-priced-per-invoice an extrapolation of a shipped per-invoice mechanism, not of nothing. What a B claim needs: a buyer or supplier agent (or AP2 mandate) choosing the early-payment rate per invoice, or a platform moving from daily batch to continuous pricing.
