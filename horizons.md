# Horizons

The index of the far view. One paragraph per live scenario in `scenarios/`, grouped by year and by the question the scenarios answer, so that scenarios that cannot both be true sit next to each other. Each paragraph says what the scenario pictures, which defended claims it stands on, the signposts that would show it arriving or failing with their clocks, and which scenario or claim it is set against. Scores are the scenario scorer's last kept net figure (seven dimensions out of 14, minus a length charge); every live scenario scores 12 to 14 of 14 and the same unchanged text has moved by a full point between runs, so compare them loosely. Revised by worker D in 0016D from the 16 live scenarios, the 26 live claims, and themes.md.

What changed since 0015D. Four scenarios came in and four went out. In 2031 the orchestrator that re-plans the close around people outside finance and the private equity sponsor buying one close for its portfolio were restored, and a model provider buying the consulting half of a large US accounting firm was added; the auditor's agent re-preparing the close, the EU listing close agents as high-risk AI, and the French tax authority accrediting invoice agents were retired at the cap. In 2036 the scenario in which banks stop asking private companies for audits replaced the rule that auditors' and clients' agents use different model families, and it now names the new defended claim it argues with. Anchors moved off the retired blind-sample claim everywhere. Three consequences to watch. 2031 has no scenario in which a regulator or tax authority moves first. The far view and the thesis now openly disagree on cost of capital. And one 2036 scenario still names the retired claim that the junior seat is going at audit firms too.

## The near view, 2027 to 2029, from the defended claims alone

No scenario is set before 2031, so the near view is the thesis itself. Agents prepare reconciliations, accruals, and variance commentary with a person approving each item; reviewers step in where the agent shows low confidence and pass what it marks confident, so the honest error rate comes from a monthly blind sample drawn from the confident items, which is also the junior's training queue. Agents stop and wait for a finance reviewer when blocked and ask no one outside finance, so the preparer chases receipts during the month and the controller still sends the year-end cutoff request to departments. Where an outside party answers for a silent company, waiting is a decision: in Mexico a buyer that does not answer a supplier's invoice cancellation within three business days has accepted it, so the payables clerk works the agent's undecided requests daily and the controller sets the default to rejection for paid invoices. The checklist survives with agents signing lines and attaching output, and public companies begin to name an owner for each agent, as the internal-control framework's 2026 guidance on generative AI asks. Auditors extend testing on agent-prepared work and run their own agents over whole populations; in fieldwork the controller reconciles the auditor's exception list against the agent's treatments, clears flagged exceptions weekly, the preparer ties the extract to ledger totals, and the auditor's screening agent returns uploads as they arrive. No audit fee reduction can appear before the 2027 audit, and none for a control in which a model judges each item if the model changed across the two audited years; the first test is one major provider's retirement of a widely used model on 14 January 2027, inside calendar-2026 fieldwork, and the controller reads the vendor's own bridge letter for it at year-end. Agents run under a human ERP role minus payee bank fields and payment release; Microsoft's roadmap through March 2027 plans restrictions that apply only when a user acts through an agent. Hard limits sit where an outside party records the act: payment release, period lock, and, in clearance countries, invoice issuance; in France, from September 2026 for large and mid-size companies and September 2027 for smaller ones, the billing clerk starts the day with the agent's drafted invoices held before release, while Italy, clearing every invoice since 2019, shows that holding them is a choice rather than a requirement. In Poland and the Czech Republic payables agents can check a new supplier account against the tax authority's register; elsewhere the clerk still calls back and logs it. In India small borrowers are underwritten from tax returns and bank feeds, so the accountant's month ends when the summary return locks on the 20th. Elsewhere lenders that take live logins to the borrower's systems still require the audit. At unaudited US companies, outsourced firms on fixed fees run agent closes reviewed by exception, planned on the lender's thirty-day package, and the client owner's monthly acceptance of entries before they post is what lets the firm also sign the lender's review. The first two-year US licensing cohorts finish in Texas (August 2028) and California (January 2029); the large UK audit firms' own reports show audit intake holding, so the junior shortage is, for now, a company problem.

## 2031

### Who first relies on the agent's work from outside: the vendor's attested rate, the crime insurer, or the lender reading the authority's record

These three cannot all be the first route by which an outside party relies on, or sets a rule for, an agent's work. The group used to hold six; the auditor's re-preparation, the EU's product rules, and the tax authority's accreditation were retired at the cap, so no regulator answer remains.

The close vendor is the audited party (9.44, the lowest in 2031). Auditors and lenders rely on the close vendor's attested error rate per task class the way they rely on a payroll processor's controls report, and mid-market companies buy an assured close and stop configuring their own; the vendor holds one model version per report period on weights it hosts itself, and its clean control opinion gives each customer the reduced-audit route in its first year. It stands on the confident-band blind sample, the auditor's discount, reliance through the route for automated controls, acceptance pricing, the two-audited-years model claim, and controller monitoring. Signposts: a close vendor's controls report covering an agent feature for a period ending in 2028 or 2029; it fails if by the end of 2030 no vendor report scopes agent error rates. It is set against the lender reading the authority's record and the crime insurer. [C51, C25, C14, C28, C42, C11]

The crime insurer licenses the payables agent (11.80, the highest in 2031). The crime insurer, not the auditor or the ERP, decides whether a payables agent may act on a changed supplier bank account, because social engineering cover already pays only if an employee verified the instruction by a logged callback, and carriers rewrite that clause to admit an agent only with a yearly agent certification and a bank name match. The controller learns what the agent may do at the insurance renewal. It stands on the payment-fraud claim, the payee-register claim, the outside-record rule, and the auditor's discount. Signposts: by the 2028 renewals a crime carrier's application asks whether an agent can change supplier bank details; it fails if carriers extend absolute AI exclusions to crime cover by 2029. It is set against the vendor's attested rate: one first outsider prices manipulation, the other error. [C23, C40, C10, C25]

The lender reads the authority's record (11.54). In an invoice-clearance country a mid-market lender reads the borrower's sales, purchases, and cash from the tax authority and the banks, so the borrower's close stops producing the numbers and starts disputing the record, and credit agreements add a cure period for errors only a counterparty can correct. It stands on the Indian lender claim, the outside-record claim, the add-back review, and the lender's monthly clock. The new defended claim that lenders elsewhere add ledger access to the audit rather than replace it is a warning to it, though not a contradiction, since the authority's record is not the borrower's ledger. Signposts: a French or Polish direct lender monitoring from e-invoicing data within a year of the September 2027 small-company deadline; it fails if tax-secrecy or data-protection rulings block delegated lender access. It is set against the vendor's attested rate. [C41, C7, C29, C19]

### Who runs the small and mid-sized company's close: the accounting firm, the ERP's own agents, the sponsor, or the model provider

Four of the eight 2031 scenarios name the accounting-firm scenario as their rival; the other three in this group do not argue with each other.

The accounting firm runs the small company's close (10.37). The small company's close is run by its CPA firm's agents rather than by anyone it employs; the firm's accountant works exceptions across about sixty clients (illustrative), and the owner's monthly acceptance sitting, a pre-filled approval of the kind the defended claims warn is anchored, is the one finance task left inside the company. Growing companies put off their first audit because the auditor's own agent is where the saving disappears. It stands on the outsourced-close claim, the auditor's own population agent, and the pre-filled-answer claim. Signposts: the 2028 and 2030 benchmark surveys of outsourced accounting showing fixed-fee share and clients per professional rising together; Norwegian evidence of no bunching below the audit-exemption line already counts against the audit-avoidance step. It is set against the ERP metering scenario. [C45, C46, C32]

The ERP meters outside agents (10.14). ERP vendors meter every write an outside agent makes through their protocol servers, so the general-purpose agent close becomes uneconomic and the close stays with the ERP's own agents and the close platforms big enough to pay the meter. ERPs already limit outside calls by concurrent requests or daily caps, so the scenario needs only a price per write. It stands on the outside-record rule, the payment-fraud claim, the checklist claim, and the ERP tool list. Signposts: an ERP vendor announcing metered pricing for protocol-server writes at or after the 2027 renewals; it fails if by 2029 the major ERPs keep outside writes free. It is set against the accounting firm, which absorbs any meter in a fixed fee. [C10, C23, C33, C39]

The sponsor runs one close for the portfolio (9.97). A private equity sponsor with forty portfolio companies (illustrative) buys one agent close from a single outsourced finance provider; each company keeps a chief financial officer and one or two people who answer the agents, the sponsor's portfolio finance lead accepts every company's agent decisions in one weekly queue, and at the 2031 refinancings lenders require a reviewer that neither the sponsor nor the provider controls, because the person accepting the books works for the owner. It stands on the outsourced-close claim, the checklist claim's named owners, and the lender's monthly clock. Signposts: by 2029 a large sponsor announces a portfolio-wide close contract; it fails if by the 2031 refinancings portfolio companies still each run their own close. It is set against the accounting firm: there the owner's own firm runs the close, here the owner is a sponsor. [C45, C33, C19]

The model provider buys the accounting firm (10.92). A model provider owns the consulting half of a large US accounting firm, bought from its private equity owners at exit, so the agents that keep thousands of small companies' books and the model they run on belong to one company, while the partners keep the attest half because a firm whose agents post entries cannot also review them. Owning the preparer lets the provider pin its own clients' model through each audit year while moving all other customers on its retirement schedule, and independent firms running closes on the same models now buy from a competitor. It stands on the outsourced-close claim and the model-retirement claim. Clocks: private equity entered large firms through the split ownership structure from 2021 to 2024, and five-to-seven-year holds put the first exits between 2028 and 2031; the ethics committee's rule on investors' control of non-attest entities is fixed by about 2028. Signposts: by 2030 a model provider owns an accounting firm's advisory business; it fails if the 2028 to 2031 exits all go to other investors or back to partners. It is set against the accounting firm. It and the sponsor scenario can both be true, which the far view has not yet resolved. [C45, C38]

### Where the close's slow step sits

The critical path runs outside finance (11.04). An orchestrator re-plans the close every hour around the people outside finance who hold its missing inputs, so managers in sales, procurement, and legal carry close-response deadlines in their annual objectives, and the controller manages the company's answers rather than its accounting. Today's task managers only block a task until its predecessor closes and send a delinquency email, so the first product that re-dates the plan when an input is late is a release that can be watched for. It stands on the claim that agents wait for a finance reviewer and the checklist claim. Signposts: by the 2028 year-end close a vendor ships re-planning against an external deadline; it fails if surveys after the 2029 close show delays still sit in finance review. It is set against the accounting firm: there the slow step is one owner's monthly sitting, here a dozen managers at a $200 million company. [C32, C33]

## 2036

### What holds the company's policy and books: the close layer, the bank, the counterparties' agents, or the standard-setter's code

The close layer owns the ledger (10.62). The close platform has become the company's control layer for every agent that touches money, holding the policy, the limits, and the identities used with banks, tax authorities, lenders, and other companies' agents, while the ERP is an interchangeable posting engine; today's treasury payment hub, with rule-based approvals, quarantine, and signatory management across ERPs, is its template. It stands on the outside-record rule, the payment-fraud claim, and the outside-record claim. Signposts: a close vendor holding agent identities used with a bank or tax authority by 2029; it fails if ERPs ship agent principals with amount, period, and payee scopes before 2030, and Microsoft's planned agent-only restrictions are the first test. It is set against the standard-setter's code. [C10, C23, C7]

The standard-setter ships the rule as code (10.38). Standard-setters publish each new standard's recognition and measurement rules as an executable reference rule set with test cases, so an effective date becomes a software release that changes every company's close agent on one day, the company records departures with its reasons, and the auditor reviews the departures list. It stands on the frozen-rule claim, the routes claim, and the model-retirement claim. Signposts: the new presentation standard takes effect in 2027, the first major standard adopted with close agents in use; by 2029 an audit regulator or firm reporting inconsistent automated application of a new standard across clients; it fails if by 2033 setters still publish only text and tagging taxonomies. It is set against the close layer: whose rules, the company's or the setter's. [C14, C26, C38]

The bank keeps the small company's ledger (11.42, the highest in 2036). Small companies no longer buy a ledger, because the bank that holds the operating account keeps the books from the payments and e-invoices it already handles; the outside accountant reviews the bank's books, and the bank still buys that outside review, because a lender that can read the books adds access to assurance rather than replacing it. One UK bank already sells a ledger and plans to let accountants post journals in it. It stands on the outside-record claim, the Indian lender claim, the payee-register claim, the outsourced-close claim, and the new lender-access claim. Signposts: by 2029 a large bank offering an agent that posts non-cash month-end entries for business customers; it fails if by 2032 small companies still buy accounting software separately at 2025 rates. It is set against the close layer. [C7, C41, C40, C45, C56, C23]

The payment date is priced per invoice (10.42). In the EU the payment date of a business invoice is a price the buyer's and supplier's agents settle for each cleared invoice, within the controller's daily limit, because once an issued invoice cannot be edited the payment date is the one term left to bargain; working capital becomes a market and the bank loses short-term lending to its customers' customers. One platform already prices early payment per invoice, but people or the platform set the rate in a daily batch. It stands on the outside-record claim, the payment-fraud claim, the outside-record rule, and the French invoice claim. Signposts: euro instant payments since October 2025 and the EU's cross-border e-invoicing from July 2030; by 2031 a discounting platform offering per-invoice agent pricing on cleared invoices; it fails if by 2034 early payment is still offered only as treasury campaigns or bank supply-chain finance. It is set against the bank keeping the ledger, over who finances the small supplier. It is the only scenario in which companies' agents bargain with each other. [C7, C23, C10, C50]

### What the audit is for: the board's own agent, or nothing the lender needs

The audit committee runs its own ledger agent (10.45). The audit committee of a listed company runs its own agent over the ledger through internal audit, reporting to the directors, so the external auditor tests the board's agent for routine classes instead of running its own, keeping its own run for the high-judgment areas the international standard bars it from leaving to internal audit; the board's agent also reports where it agrees with management's, and the auditor samples those agreed entries. It stands on the auditor's own population agent, the claim that agreement between agents is not evidence, and the confident-band blind sample, and contradicts the auditor's-own-agent claim directly. Signposts: by 2030 a proxy statement disclosing an audit-committee-commissioned agent review of the ledger; it fails if through 2034 audit firms keep running their own population agents and committees buy nothing. Its old rival, the different-model-families rule, is retired, so it is the only live answer on where the listed company's audit gets its independence. [C46, C49, C51]

Lenders stop asking private companies for audits (10.56). US banks replace the loan size at which they demand an audit with a demand for ledger access and the accounting firm's measured error rate by task class, so the private-company audit shrinks to a product for sales and capital raising and audit firms' mid-market practices lose their largest source of work. Each bank already sets its own audit line, the Federal Reserve's manual leaves the minimum to the bank, and a board can move it by vote. It stands on the outsourced-close claim, the auditor's own agent (which keeps audits expensive while reviews get cheaper), and the lender's monthly clock. It argues with the new defended claim that lenders add access to the audit because the audit lowers the rate, and its stated reason is that review and audit quality are already indistinguishable, so a measured error rate may buy what the audit signals. Signposts: by the 2031 refinancing vintage a lender advertises an audit alternative based on ledger access; it fails if private-company audit counts hold or rise through 2034, or if a filed term loan pairs system access with an audit requirement, as one asset-based loan already does. [C45, C46, C19; contradicts C56]

### Where judgment comes from: juniors hired to attack, or a rented signature

Juniors are hired to attack the agents (10.62). A first-year accountant is hired to attack the company's finance agents rather than to prepare entries, because the yearly agent certifications insurers and auditors accept require adversarial testing by people who know how books are fooled, and licensing boards count that as experience. Red-teaming AI is today a senior security job, so the work has to split. It still names the retired claim that the junior seat is going at audit firms as well as companies; the audit firms' own intake figures retired that claim, and the scenario needs repointing to the company-side hiring claim, which carries the same step for companies. It also stands on the payment-fraud claim, the routes claim, and the seat claim. Signposts: by 2029 an audit firm or close vendor advertising finance-specific red-teaming; it fails if by 2032 attack testing is fully automated by vendors' own agents. It is set against the controller of record. [C23, C26, C30; C53 retired, repoint to C17]

The controller of record (10.71). Companies under about $250 million of revenue no longer employ a controller, because the job has become a licensed signature over the agents' settings and error rates that one outside professional provides for twenty or thirty companies at once. It stands on the checklist claim, the seat claim, the hiring claim, and the outsourced-close claim. Signposts: a 2031 covenant naming an outside controller of record; a licensing board proposal on supervision ratios; it fails if small companies keep an in-house controller through 2034 while juniors are still not hired. It is set against the juniors scenario: there in-house judgment is rebuilt, here it is rented because it was never grown. [C33, C30, C17, C45]

## Not covered by any live scenario

- A regulator or tax authority moving first in 2031. Both such scenarios (the EU listing close agents as high-risk AI, the French authority accrediting invoice agents) were retired at the cap within one run's noise of the scenarios that replaced them; their files remain as drafts.
- Agents answering other companies' agents under default rules. The Mexican cancellation claim, where silence is acceptance after three business days, anchors no scenario; the payment-date scenario is the only one with agents on both sides, and it is about price, not defaults.
- Audit reorganized around two agents. With the re-preparation and different-models scenarios retired, no scenario pictures the auditor's agent and the client's agent disagreeing, and the claim that agreement is not evidence anchors only the audit-committee scenario.
- A provider outage that stops many closes at once (draft below the bar in the model thread).
- Judgments reserved by law to a licensed person (draft held in the agent-when-blocked thread; no regulator has moved toward it).
- Review packs, and the portfolio close changing hands at exit (two 2036 drafts held in the sponsor thread).
- Anchor load. The payment-fraud claim and the outsourced-close claim each anchor six scenarios, the cap. The Mexican claim anchors none; the monitoring, agreement, French invoice, and lender-access claims anchor one each.
