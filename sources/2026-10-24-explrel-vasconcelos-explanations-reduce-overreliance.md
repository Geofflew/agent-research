# Vasconcelos et al., Explanations Can Reduce Overreliance on AI Systems During Decision-Making (CSCW 2023)

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06823
Type: primary
Read in session: 0009C

Summary: Five studies (N = 731) in a maze task with a simulated AI. Overreliance did not fall with explanations in prior work, but fell when verifying the AI was cheaper (easier task or explanation) or pay was higher. Counter-evidence to a flat explanation-anchoring claim: anchoring depends on the cost of checking. Lab task, non-professionals, abstract only checked.

Quotes:
- "overreliance, when people agree with an AI, even when it is incorrect" — Vasconcelos et al., abstract
- "overreliance does not reduce when the AI produces explanations for its predictions, compared to only providing predictions" — Vasconcelos et al., abstract
- "people strategically choose whether or not to engage with an AI explanation" — Vasconcelos et al., abstract
- "Through 5 studies (N = 731)" — Vasconcelos et al., abstract
- "some of the null effects found in literature could be due in part to the explanation not sufficiently reducing the costs of verifying the AI's prediction" — Vasconcelos et al., abstract
