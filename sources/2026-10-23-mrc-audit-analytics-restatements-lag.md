# Audit Analytics: Restatements, where they come from (2013 blog)

URL: https://blog.auditanalytics.com/restatements-where-they-come-from/
Type: vendor
Read in session: 0005A

Summary: Audit Analytics blog, 2012 data. Avg restatement covers 534 days = ~1.5 years of data wrong before found.
Most unintentional errors found in annual audit (38% of 768 disclosed Feb-Apr). SEC comment letters another trigger.
Vendor blog, old data. Ground-truth signal for close errors slow, tied to audit cycle, not per-item.

Quotes:
- "an absolute value of the indicator averaged at 534 days. This means that companies had to correct an average of a year and a half of financial data." — Olga Usvyatsky, Audit Analytics blog, Aug 2 2013
- "if the errors went undetected for so long, how were they finally discovered?" — Olga Usvyatsky, Audit Analytics blog, Aug 2 2013
- "Most unintentional errors are discovered during annual audits." — Olga Usvyatsky, Audit Analytics blog, Aug 2 2013
- "out of the 768 restatements covered in the 2012 Audit Analytics restatement report, 295 restatements, or 38%, were disclosed during this period" — Olga Usvyatsky, Audit Analytics blog, Aug 2 2013
- "in 2012 alone, 74 companies explicitly stated in their comment letters that they would restate financial statements as a result of discussions with the SEC" — Olga Usvyatsky, Audit Analytics blog, Aug 2 2013
- "only 49 companies indicated in their restatements disclosure that the SEC was in any way involved in the restatement process" — Olga Usvyatsky, Audit Analytics blog, Aug 2 2013
- "thousands are discovered every year, but most are not material enough even to warrant a disclosure" — Olga Usvyatsky, Audit Analytics blog, Aug 2 2013
- "in 2012, only 11 restatements were related to intentional misrepresentation of data" — Olga Usvyatsky, Audit Analytics blog, Aug 2 2013
