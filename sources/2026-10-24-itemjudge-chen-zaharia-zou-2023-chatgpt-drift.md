# Chen, Zaharia and Zou, How is ChatGPT's behavior changing over time? (arXiv 2307.09009 v3)

URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2307.09009v3
Type: primary
Read in session: 0008A

Summary: Compares GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 March 2023 vs June 2023 on eight tasks. Same-named service shifted in both directions: GPT-4 prime test 84.0% to 51.1% (GPT-3.5 49.6% to 76.2%); GPT-4 directly executable code 52.0% to 10.0%; GPT-4 multi-hop better, GPT-3.5 worse. Authors tie much of it to changed instruction-following. Shows per-item judgment tasks can change without notice between versions.

Quotes:
- "GPT-4's accuracy dropped from 84.0% in March to 51.1% in June, and there was a large improvement of GPT-3.5's accuracy, from 49.6% to 76.2%" — Chen, Zaharia and Zou
- "the number of directly executable generations dropped from March to June" — Chen, Zaharia and Zou
- "over 50% generations of GPT-4 were directly executable in March, but only 10% in June" — Chen, Zaharia and Zou
- "the percentage of generations that are directly executable dropped from 52.0% in March to 10.0% in June" — Chen, Zaharia and Zou
- "We provide evidence that GPT-4's ability to follow user instructions has decreased over time" — Chen, Zaharia and Zou
- "the behavior of the "same" LLM service can change substantially in a relatively short amount of time, highlighting the need for continuous monitoring of LLMs" — Chen, Zaharia and Zou
- "CoT did not help the June version of GPT-4 much: the accuracy was actually 0.1% worse" — Chen, Zaharia and Zou
- "its average verbosity (number of generated characters) decreased from 638.3 in March to 3.9 in June" — Chen, Zaharia and Zou
- "GPT-4 performed better at multi-hop questions in June than in March, while GPT-3.5's performance dropped on this task" — Chen, Zaharia and Zou
