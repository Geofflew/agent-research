# IESBA Snapshot: Ethics and Independence Approach to the Use of Technology (June 2026)

URL: https://www.ethicsboard.org/news-events/2026-06/iesba-snapshot-ethics-and-independence-approach-use-technology
Type: secondary
Read in session: 0003F

Summary: IESBA's own plain-language Q&A on the 2023 technology revisions (effective December 2024). States that responsibility cannot be delegated to a machine, that opaque outputs are unacceptable, and that IESBA has chosen not to pursue further Code changes for now, relying on existing principles. Admits agentic AI raises open questions on traceability, human oversight and accountability, and that governance lags adoption. Plans an overarching technology guidance publication (Q3 2026) and an AI-specific one (Q4 2026).

Quotes:
- "professional responsibility cannot be delegated to a machine." — IESBA Snapshot, Q4, automation bias
- "Relying on opaque or unexplained outputs is not acceptable." — IESBA Snapshot, Q4, professional competence and due care
- "IESBA has deliberately chosen not to pursue additional Code revisions currently." — IESBA Snapshot, Q5
- "Approved in April 2023 and effective from December 2024" — IESBA Snapshot, Q4
- "IESBA has established a standing Technology Expert Group of eight practitioners" — IESBA Snapshot, Q3
- "AI-Specific NAM (planned Q4 2026)." — IESBA Snapshot, Q6, non-authoritative guidance timetable
- "raise additional risk concerns, including critical questions about traceability, human oversight, and accountability." — IESBA Snapshot, Q7, on agentic systems
- "but governance and oversight mechanisms lag behind adoption." — IESBA Snapshot, Q7
