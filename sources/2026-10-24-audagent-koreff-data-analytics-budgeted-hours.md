# Are auditors' reliance on conclusions from data analytics impacted by different data analytic inputs? (Koreff, J. Information Systems 2022)

URL: https://digitalcommons.trinity.edu/busadmin_faculty/140
Type: primary
Read in session: 0008B

Summary: Experimental study of auditors' budgeted hours after analytics conclusions. Budget response depends on model type (anomaly vs predictive) and data type (financial vs nonfinancial), so analytics do not uniformly cut or raise hours. Exceptions found can raise hours, which bears on whether full-population testing lowers fees.

Quotes:
- "when financial data is analyzed auditors increase budgeted audit hours more when predictive models are used than when anomaly models are used" — Koreff, abstract
- "data analytics with different inputs do not uniformly impact auditors' judgments" — Koreff, abstract
- "auditors may not uniformly incorporate this information into their decision making" — Koreff, abstract
