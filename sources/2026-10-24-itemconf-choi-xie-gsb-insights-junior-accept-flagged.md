# Stanford GSB Insights on Choi and Xie, "Human + AI in Accounting" (senior vs junior response to AI confidence)

URL: https://gsb.stanford.edu/insights/ai-reshaping-accounting-jobs-doing-boring-stuff
Type: secondary
Read in session: 0008A

Summary: School write-up of the Choi and Xie study (JAR 2026; 277 accountants, 79 SME firms). Adds what the abstract lacks: seniors step in when AI confidence drops, but juniors accept outputs even when flagged uncertain. No accuracy numbers for confident-but-wrong AI; full paper (SSRN/Stanford/Wiley) not reachable as text, AEA registry lists a planned ~300-accountant framed experiment only.

Quotes:
- "Senior accountants tend to treat AI as a collaborator. They are more discerning, stepping in when the system's confidence drops and applying human oversight where it's most needed" — Stanford GSB Insights article
- "Junior staff, on the other hand, are more likely to accept AI-generated outputs at face value, even when those outputs are flagged as uncertain" — Stanford GSB Insights article
- "The study provides early evidence on potentially AI-generated errors flowing through human-in-the-loop accounting systems" — Stanford GSB Insights article
- "In Choi and Xie's survey, 62% said they were concerned about AI-generated errors" — Stanford GSB Insights article
- "accountants who use AI support more clients per week and finalize monthly statements 7.5 days faster" — Stanford GSB Insights article
- "More complex areas like audit, tax strategy, and valuation are largely untouched" — Stanford GSB Insights article
