# Lyngstadas and Mauritzen: auditor and dividends in small firms (Norway audit exemption)

URL: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2301.11079
Type: primary
Read in session: 0003F

Summary: Working paper (2023) using Norwegian register data and a regression discontinuity at the 2011 small-firm audit exemption. Tests for firms shrinking revenue to stay under the threshold and finds no bunching. Only 14% of eligible-firm-years forgo an audit. Cites audit cost of 6% or more of revenue for European SMEs. Norway not UK; the exemption is a size threshold, so it speaks to bunching generally but not to US lender-driven demand.

Quotes:
- "No pattern of bunching is apparent." — Figure 3 caption, operating revenue histogram around the exemption threshold
- "an audit can cost 6% or more among private small- and medium-sized European firms." — authors citing Kausar et al. (2016)
- "and choose to forgo an audit is 14% of the total" — sample description, share of firms eligible and opting out
- "We consistently find that forgoing auditing led to a significant increase in dividends in small private firms." — abstract
