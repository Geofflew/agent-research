# Briggs (2003), An Examination of Decision Aid Reliance in a Dynamic Environment (Virginia Tech doctoral dissertation, abstract page)

URL: https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/items/6cef76fe-6b23-4baf-94df-ca5236e14dbd
Type: primary
Read in session: 0008B

Summary: Lab experiment, managerial accounting overhead-cost prediction, accounting/IS students (172 recruited, 130 analysed per the PDF). Aid's accuracy is degraded mid-task (error about 230% of original). Subjects kept relying on it and did worse than unaided subjects (mean error 15,497 aided vs 13,908 unaided, N=32 vs 35, p=.042, last 8 trials; PDF Table 8). Feedback on aid vs own accuracy mitigated over-reliance. Students, not company staff; no seeded errors in a reviewed entry.

Quotes:
- "In the experimental scenario, a decision support system's recommended solutions become inaccurate due to a shift in environmental conditions." — Briggs, abstract
- "The results of the study provide evidence that decision-makers rely on decision aids, and are susceptible to over-reliance on them." — Briggs, abstract
- "there is evidence that feedback environment can help reliance and mitigate over-reliance" — Briggs, abstract
- "Despite this fact, studies have shown that reliance upon these aids is incomplete, even when they process data in a highly efficient manner." — Briggs, abstract
