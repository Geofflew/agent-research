# Zhang, Liao and Bellamy, Effect of Confidence and Explanation on Accuracy and Trust Calibration in AI-Assisted Decision Making (FAT* 2020)

URL: https://arxiv.org/html/2001.02114v1
Type: primary
Read in session: 0008A

Summary: Two human experiments (72 participants in Exp 1). Showing the AI confidence score calibrated trust: people switched to the AI more when confidence was above 80% and less below 60%. Joint accuracy did not improve; human and AI uncertainty were correlated, so humans were no better than the AI on low-confidence cases. Caveat: authors say result could change if human and model knowledge were less correlated.

Quotes:
- "we show that confidence score can help calibrate people's trust in an AI model, but trust calibration alone is not sufficient to improve AI-assisted decision making" — Zhang, Liao and Bellamy
- "when the AI's confidence level was high—above 80%—participants' trust was significantly enhanced by seeing the confidence scores" — Zhang, Liao and Bellamy
- "We originally expected that the AI-assisted prediction (i.e. human-AI joint decision) would be more accurate than the AI alone when the AI confidence was low, but that did not turn out to be true" — Zhang, Liao and Bellamy
- "The fact that showing confidence improved trust and trust calibration but failed to improve the AI-assisted accuracy is puzzling" — Zhang, Liao and Bellamy
- "the participants' own accuracy was 65%" — Zhang, Liao and Bellamy
- "while the AI accuracy was 75%" — Zhang, Liao and Bellamy
