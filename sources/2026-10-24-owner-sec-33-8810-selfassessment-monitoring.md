# SEC Release 33-8810, Commission Guidance Regarding Management's Report on ICFR (Federal Register text, 27 Jun 2007): on-going monitoring and self-assessment

URL: https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2007-06-27/html/E7-12299.htm
Type: primary
Read in session: 0009C

Summary: SEC interpretive guidance says management's evidence on control operation comes from direct tests, on-going monitoring, or both. On-going monitoring includes self-assessment, which can be done by the personnel who operate the control, but that evidence is weaker because of lower objectivity. Premise for an agent-settings sub-certification: the operator's own attestation counts as evidence but is discounted, and higher ICFR risk calls for more objective evaluators or direct testing. Quotes:
- "The evidence management evaluates comes from direct tests of controls, on-going monitoring, or a combination of both." — SEC, Release 33-8810, Section II.B (evidence)
- "On-going monitoring includes management's normal, recurring activities that provide information about the operation of controls." — SEC, Release 33-8810, Section II.B (evidence)
- "Self-assessment is a broad term that can refer to different types of procedures performed by individuals with varying degrees of objectivity." — SEC, Release 33-8810, Section II.B (evidence)
- "It includes assessments made by the personnel who operate the control as well as members of management who are not responsible for operating the control." — SEC, Release 33-8810, Section II.B (evidence)
- "evidence from self-assessments performed by personnel responsible for operating the control generally provides less evidence due to the evaluator's lower degree of objectivity." — SEC, Release 33-8810, Section II.B (evidence)
- "When ICFR risk is assessed as high, the evidence management obtains would ordinarily consist of direct testing or on-going monitoring activities performed by individuals who have a higher degree of objectivity." — SEC, Release 33-8810, Section II.B (evidence)
