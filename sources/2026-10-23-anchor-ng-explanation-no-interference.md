# The Effects of Fraud Risk and Management Representation on Auditors' Hypothesis Generation (Ng, Green, Simnett 2001)

URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/abacus/v37y2001i3p352-368.html
Type: primary
Read in session: 0005B

Summary: Abacus 2001 between-subjects experiment. Counter-evidence: a non-misstatement mgmt explanation did NOT reduce auditors' hypothesis generation; steered new hypotheses toward same cycle as explanation. Explanation shapes WHERE auditors look, not whether.

Quotes:
- "The availability of a non‐misstatement management explanation was found not to interfere with auditors' hypothesis generation performance, but facilitated the generation of proportionately more misstatement and fraud hypotheses from the same transaction cycle as that indicated by the management explanation." — abstract
- "auditors are sensitive to increased fraud risk by generating more fraud hypotheses, while the number of misstatement hypotheses generated is not affected by fraud risk" — abstract
