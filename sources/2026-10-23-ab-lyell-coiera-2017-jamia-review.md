# Lyell and Coiera 2017, Automation bias and verification complexity (JAMIA, PMC)

URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7651899/
Type: primary
Read in session: 0006A

Summary: JAMIA 2017 review, 40 of 890 papers. AB occurs in single tasks, diagnosis-type, high verification complexity; not only multitasking. Tied to cognitive load. Mitigate by cutting load. Literature fragmented, few control-condition tests.

Quotes:
- "contrary to the prevailing human factors view, ab was found in single tasks, typically involving diagnosis rather than monitoring, and with high verification complexity." — Lyell and Coiera, abstract results
- "ab appears to be associated with the degree of cognitive load experienced in decision tasks, and appears to not be uniquely associated with multitasking." — abstract conclusion
- "of 890 papers identified, 40 met the inclusion criteria; 6 were in health care." — abstract results
- "few studies reported the statistical significance of ab compared to a control condition." — abstract limitations
- "strategies to minimize ab might focus on cognitive load reduction." — abstract conclusion
