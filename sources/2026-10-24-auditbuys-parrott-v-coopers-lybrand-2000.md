# Parrott v. Coopers & Lybrand (N.Y. 2000) — restates Credit Alliance test

URL: https://www.law.cornell.edu/nyctap/I00_0143.htm
Type: primary
Read in session: 0009B

Summary: NY Court of Appeals reapplies the Credit Alliance tripartite test and dismisses; says the privity limit rests on avoiding "limitless liability". Gives the three elements verbatim (via Prudential v. Dewey Ballantine). Confirms NY still requires near-privity for third-party reliance.

Quotes:
- "and (3) some conduct by the maker of the statement linking it to the relying party and evincing its understanding of that reliance" — Parrott v. Coopers & Lybrand (N.Y. 2000)
- "is necessary in order to provide fair and manageable bounds to what otherwise could prove to be limitless liability" — Parrott v. Coopers & Lybrand (N.Y. 2000)
- "The evidence here is insufficient to establish a relationship so close as to approach that of privity." — Parrott v. Coopers & Lybrand (N.Y. 2000)
