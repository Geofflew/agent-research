# Airbus Fly-by-Wire: A Total Approach to Dependability (Traverse, Lacaze, Souyris, 2004)

URL: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/978-1-4020-8157-6_18.pdf
Type: primary
Read in session: 0001F

Summary: Airbus engineers describe the flight control system's "error tolerance" layer: primary and secondary computers use different microprocessors, different coding tools, different design teams and different functional specifications, on top of best-effort software quality. In-service events (an A320 loss of both ELAC after a component fell short of its temperature range; an A340 single-component fault trapping all three PRIM) showed the dissimilarity was justified. They note supplier consolidation threatens independent component choice, so dissimilarity is now deliberately managed. Note the paper does not use the phrase "common-mode failure" in this section; the rationale is stated as additional protection against design and manufacturing faults.

Quotes:
- "Dissimilarity is an additional precaution that is not used to reduce the required software quality effort." — Traverse, Lacaze, Souyris, sec. 3.1.4
- "An additional protection has nevertheless been provided which consists in using two different types of computers" — Traverse et al., sec. 3.2.1
- "We therefore have two different design and manufacturing teams with different microprocessors" — Traverse et al., sec. 3.2.1
- "In-service experience has shown that PRIM/SEC dissimilarity is fully justified." — Traverse et al., sec. 3.2.3
- "we have moved from this kind of “random” dissimilarity to a managed one" — Traverse et al., sec. 3.2.3
