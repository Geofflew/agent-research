# Faria-e-Castro, Gopalan, Pal, Sanchez, Yerramilli: EBITDA Add-backs in Debt Contracting: A Step Too Far? (full-text draft)

URL: https://r.jina.ai/https://www.bauer.uh.edu/yerramilli/EBITDA_Addbacks.pdf
Type: primary
Read in session: 0007A

Summary: Author-hosted full text (Dec 2021 draft, via r.jina.ai mirror) of St. Louis Fed WP 2022-029. States add-backs inflate covenant EBITDA and understate leverage, diluting covenant trip-wire function; 4.2% per-add-back rise in 60-day delinquency probability; cites S&P ~30% projected-vs-realized EBITDA gap. Does not test accrual substitution.

Quotes:
- "This results in the covenants being defined on inflated EBITDA values and potentially helps understate the true leverage or credit risk of the borrower." — Faria-e-Castro et al., introduction
- "Therefore, covenants based on inflated measures of EBITDA, by diluting some of these protective features have the potential to hurt loan performance" — Faria-e-Castro et al., introduction
- "with every additional addback, the probability of the loan becoming 60 days delinquent increases by 4.2% within 3 years of origination" — Faria-e-Castro et al., introduction
- "it may be that lead banks use non-GAAP addbacks to hide the true financial condition of borrowers from syndicate participants, and possibly from regulators." — Faria-e-Castro et al., hypotheses (dark side)
- "exceeded actual realized EBITDA in the two calendar years following the year of origination by about 30% on average" — Faria-e-Castro et al., fn 1 citing S&P
