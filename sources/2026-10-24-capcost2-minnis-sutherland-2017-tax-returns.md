# Minnis & Sutherland (2017), Financial Statements as Monitoring Mechanisms: Evidence from Small Commercial Loans (abstract, IDEAS/RePEc)

URL: https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/joares/v55y2017i1p197-233.html
Type: primary
Read in session: 0009B

Summary: Journal of Accounting Research 55(1), March 2017. Bank loan-level data on small commercial borrowers: banks request statements for only half the loans, and business tax returns act as both substitute and complement depending on borrower and information asymmetry. Evidence that outside India, small-loan lenders already skip statements for many loans.

Quotes:
- "We find that banks request financial statements for half the loans" — Minnis & Sutherland abstract
- "tax returns can be both substitutes and complements to financial statements, conditional on borrower characteristics and the degree of bank–borrower information asymmetry" — abstract
- "Frequent financial reporting is used to monitor collateral, but only for non–real estate loans and only when the collateral is easily accessible to lenders." — abstract
