# Bily v. Arthur Young & Co., 3 Cal.4th 370 (Cal. 1992) — auditor liability to third parties

URL: https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/bily-v-arthur-young-co-31350
Type: primary
Read in session: 0009B

Summary: California Supreme Court limits auditor negligence liability to the client; non-clients (including lenders) can recover only as specifically intended beneficiaries known to the auditor, via negligent misrepresentation under Restatement (Second) of Torts §552. Rejects foreseeability test. Opinion also summarizes Credit Alliance, where a lender relying on audited statements lost in one case and won in the other because of direct auditor-lender communication.

Quotes:
- "we hold that an auditor's liability for general negligence in the conduct of an audit of its client financial statements is confined to the client" — Bily v. Arthur Young & Co., 3 Cal.4th 370 (Cal. 1992)
- "Such persons are specifically intended beneficiaries of the audit report who are known to the auditor and for whose benefit it renders the audit report." — Bily v. Arthur Young & Co., 3 Cal.4th 370 (Cal. 1992)
- "An auditor may, however, be held liable for negligent misrepresentations in an audit report to those persons who act in reliance upon those misrepresentations in a transaction which the auditor intended to influence" — Bily v. Arthur Young & Co., 3 Cal.4th 370 (Cal. 1992)
- "the primary, if not exclusive, "end and aim" of the audits in the second case was to satisfy the lender" — Bily v. Arthur Young & Co., 3 Cal.4th 370 (Cal. 1992)
