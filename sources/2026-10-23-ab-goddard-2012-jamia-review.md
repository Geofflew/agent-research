# Goddard, Roudsari, Wyatt 2012, Automation bias: systematic review (JAMIA, PMC)

URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3240751/
Type: primary
Read in session: 0006A

Summary: JAMIA 2012 systematic review, 74 of 13821 papers. AB = over-accept DSS output. Erroneous advice raised wrong-decision risk 26% in meta-analysis of 4 papers. Mediators: experience, trust, workload, complexity. Mitigators: training, accountability, advice position, confidence levels, info not recommendation.

Quotes:
- "of 13 821 retrieved papers, 74 met the inclusion criteria." — Goddard et al., abstract
- "mitigators of ab included implementation factors such as training and emphasizing user accountability, and dss design factors such as the position of advice on the screen, updated confidence levels attached to dss output, and the provision of information versus recommendation." — abstract
- "environmental mediators included workload, task complexity, and time constraint, which pressurized cognitive resources." — abstract
- "the occasional incorrect advice they give may tempt users to reverse a correct decision they have already made, and thus introduce errors of over-reliance." — introduction
- "ab manifests in errors of commission (following incorrect advice) and omission (failing to act because of not being prompted to do so) when using cdss." — introduction
- "friedman et al 7 noticed that in 6% of cases, clinicians over-rode their own correct decisions in favor of erroneous advice from the dss." — introduction
- "when in error the cdss increased the risk of an incorrect decision being made by 26%." — results, meta-analysis of four papers (risk ratio 1.26)
