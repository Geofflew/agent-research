# Credit Alliance Corp. v. Andersen & Co., 65 N.Y.2d 536 (N.Y. 1985) — opinion text (vLex copy)

Type: primary
Read in session: 0009B

Summary: NY Court of Appeals opinion. Lenders who conditioned financing on an audited statement lost a negligence claim against the auditor for lack of privity or its equivalent; a second lender (European American Bank) with direct, sustained auditor communications survived. Page text is truncated by paywall after the facts (tripartite test appears in Bily and Parrott entries). Fact pattern shows the lender demanded an audit as a condition of credit.

Quotes:
- "plaintiffs advised Smith that as a condition to extending additional major financing, they would insist upon examining an audited financial statement." — Credit Alliance Corp. v. Andersen & Co., 65 N.Y.2d 536 (N.Y. 1985)
- "The critical issue common to these two appeals is whether an accountant may be held liable, absent privity of contract, to a party who relies to his detriment upon a negligently prepared financial report" — Credit Alliance Corp. v. Andersen & Co., 65 N.Y.2d 536 (N.Y. 1985)
- "Because the allegations in plaintiffs ' complaint and affidavit fail to set forth either a relationship of contractual privity with Andersen or a relationship sufficiently intimate to be equated with privity, the first cause of action should be dismissed." — Credit Alliance Corp. v.
