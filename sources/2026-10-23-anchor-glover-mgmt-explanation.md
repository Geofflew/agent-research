# Research Summary 6: Analytical Procedures and Audit Planning Decisions (Glover, Jiambalvo, Kennedy 2000/2001)

URL: https://www.journalofaccountancy.com/issues/2001/feb/researchsummary6analyticalproceduresandauditplanningdecisions/
Type: secondary
Read in session: 0005B

Summary: JofA summary of AJPT 2000 experiment, 67 practicing auditors. Unexpected fluctuation + mgmt explanation, minimal corroboration. Only 25% revised plans w/o mgmt incentive; 56% with incentive. >40% did not extend tests = underauditing. Ties to AICPA concern: accepting mgmt representations uncorroborated.

Quotes:
- "Sixty-seven practicing auditors participated in an experiment in which they were asked to make audit planning decisions for a hypothetical client." — study description
- "In the minimal-corroboration condition, when there was no explicit incentive for management to misstate financial results, only 25% of the auditors revised their plans." — results
- "When management had an explicit incentive and only minimal corroboration was available, only 56% increased planned tests." — results
- "The fact that more than 40% of the auditors did not extend tests, given an unexpected fluctuation and little corroboration for management's explanation, raises concerns about whether underauditing may occur even in a relatively high-risk setting." — authors
- "willingness to accept management's representations without corroboration" — AICPA 1996-97 Audit Risk Alert concern, quoted by authors
