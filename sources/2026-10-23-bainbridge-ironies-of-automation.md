# Bainbridge 1983, Ironies of Automation (Automatica 19(6))

URL: https://fermatslibrary.com/p/028c7a80
Type: primary
Read in session: 0004

Summary: Full paper text (HTML mirror, Fermat's Library). Designer automates what can be; operator left with residue + monitoring + takeover. Skills decay w/o use. More automation = more operator training need. Process-control context, not finance.

Quotes:
- "the second irony is that the designer who tries to eliminate the operator still leaves the operator to do the tasks which the designer cannot think how to automate." — Bainbridge, Introduction
- "unfortunately, physical skills deteriorate when they are not used, particularly the refinements of gain and timing." — Bainbridge, 1.1.1 Manual control skills
- "a more serious irony is that the automatic control system has been put in because it can do the job better than the operator, but yet the operator is being asked to monitor that it is working effectively." — Bainbridge, monitoring section
- "perhaps the final irony is that it is the most successful automated systems, with rare need for manual intervention, which may need the greatest investment in human operator training." — Bainbridge, end of section 2
