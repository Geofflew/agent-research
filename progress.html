Live claims

  • C7. The externally verifiable record that lenders and auditors need to check a company's numbers without the company will be assembled from mandated invoice clearance and structured payment messages that outside parties already hold, not from a filing schema or a vendor schema, so the close platform's external-facing job becomes reconciling the ledger to records the tax authority and the banks hold rather than publishing its own.
  • C10. ERP and close platforms will give agent authority the control shape of the market access rules, pre-set limits, rejection before entry, and automatic disable until a named person re-enables, only at the points where a party outside the company records the agent's act, which are payment release, period lock, and in clearance countries invoice issuance, because unlike an executed trade a journal in an open period is reversed by the company itself, so for ordinary journals the kill switch is the period status the controller already holds; and where the outside record runs a deadline with a default, as buyers' acceptance of invoices does in Chile, Colombia, and Mexico, an agent that stops for a reviewer has already acted, so there the limit is a deadline before which the agent must escalate, not a stop.
  • C11. Close agents will deliver the full-population transaction testing that continuous auditing promised since 1989 and never reached, but it will arrive as the controller's monitoring inside the close platform rather than as internal audit's tool, and its payoff will be a lower external audit budget granted only to companies with a clean prior-year control opinion, not the replacement of periodic audit.
  • C17. Close agents will shrink finance teams from the bottom by not hiring juniors rather than by cutting existing staff, turning the pyramid into a diamond during a shortage, and by about 2032 the binding constraint on agent adoption will be the supply of mid-level reviewers able to judge exceptions, because the routine preparation that trained them is the work agents take first.
  • C19. The faster close that agents make possible will not be bought for public reporting, because public reporting is moving to less frequent filing and the median close already finishes weeks inside the filing deadline, so the value of agent speed will be captured in the lender's monthly package and in cost per close, and vendors selling days-to-close will hit a ceiling.
  • C23. The hard limit on agent write authority in payables and the close will be adversarial input rather than error rate, and agent roles will exclude payee bank details and payment release, carved out of the inherited human role where the ERP's identity model inherits, as Workday's and SAP's do, and left out of a purpose-built agent permission set where it does not, as Business Central's does, because the agent reads the same vendor emails and invoices that carry payment fraud, prompt injection has no reliable prevention, and the ERP identity models give agents the permissions of human roles in which keeping bank changes from the payer is only a setting.
  • C26. Unreviewed close work will arrive by two routes sorted by task class, a frozen rule that a model drafts and the auditor benchmarks, and a threshold set in dollars against materiality as well as in confidence, checked by a sample drawn from above it, while an informal preference is not sufficient control evidence without a baseline test or an outcome sample, and every switch of a class to auto-post or change of a threshold is a configuration change that restarts the evidence.
  • C28. Close platforms selling to audited companies will price agents per reviewer-accepted task rather than by seat or by zero-touch outcome, which moves the purchase to the CFO, but because acceptance is then both the billing event and the control evidence, the vendor gains from the anchoring C51 describes, so the blind sample must sit outside the billing unit and refunds must follow its disagreement rate rather than rejections in ordinary review.
  • C29. In the lender-driven close of C19 and C45, the person's review will concentrate on the covenant EBITDA adjustment schedule rather than on the entries that feed it, because the slack in the most common covenant lives in negotiated add-backs rather than in managed entries, so the agent will post routine entries and the officer signing the compliance certificate will approve each add-back against its credit-agreement clause.
  • C30. The first-level review seat on routine close work will be removed before any validation study exists, but only the former preparer's mechanical review of agent output goes, while the conceptual review the auditor tests stays with a manager, because junior reviewers add value by catching mechanical errors, which the agent's rule-computed checks now catch, and the close has no fast outcome signal that could validate removing the manager.
  • C32. Shipped close agents stop and wait for a finance reviewer and ask no one outside finance, so today the preparer chases the missing facts, and when agents do start asking, the design already shipped for this is a pre-filled estimate the owner approves, which turns the reply into anchored inquiry that auditors treat as insufficient on its own, so the question should follow the rules medicine wrote for machine-generated queries: no proposed figure, the indicators that justify asking, a "don't know" option, and no repeat until the desired answer comes.
  • C33. Agents will sign the preparer's lines on the close checklist and leave the quarterly sub-certification chain behind the CEO's and CFO's Section 302 certifications without its bottom signer, so accountability moves from the task to the agent: the reviewer checks agent-signed lines by the output attached to them, sign-offs with no output are blocked, and each agent gets a named owner who sub-certifies its settings and measured results rather than the numbers.
  • C38. The model inside a close agent will change on the model provider's retirement schedule rather than the customer's, which makes every retirement a program change to an automated control at every customer at once, so close vendors will pin a model version for each customer's fiscal year and move customers to a new model between audits, with the controller approving the switch and the blind sample restarting.
  • C39. Which write actions an ERP's protocol server gives outside agents is the ERP vendor's choice, which no rule constrains as payment rules constrain banks, so whether a general-purpose agent or only the ERP's own agent can prepare the close is settled ledger by ledger before any question of capability, and on a read-only ledger the agent's work still reaches the books through a person or a close platform.
  • C40. Payables agents will first be allowed to enter a new vendor bank account where a tax authority publishes which accounts belong to which tax ID, as Poland and the Czech Republic do, because there the payee check that C23 leaves to a person's callback becomes a lookup in the authority-held record that C7 describes, which an injected email cannot change and a name match like EU Verification of Payee cannot replace.
  • C41. For small-business loans in India the lender already reads the borrower's tax returns and bank feed directly instead of a package the borrower prepares, so for those borrowers the close stops being an input to the loan, and the accountant's month-end job becomes making the ledger agree with the GST return before it is filed, because the filed return is what the lender reads.
  • C42. The lower external audit fee that C11 expects from agent monitoring will reach only companies whose close agent runs one model version across two consecutive audited years, because auditors cut effort only after a clean prior-year control opinion on an unchanged control while hosted model versions now live about twelve to sixteen months, so vendors selling to audited companies will run on open weights they host themselves the controls in which the model judges each item, and turn the rest into rules the model drafts and the controller freezes.
  • C45. The first closes that agents prepare and people review only by exception will be at unaudited private companies whose books a CPA firm keeps on a fixed fee, but the firm can give the lender the review report those companies need only while the client's owner accepts the agent's decisions each month, because an agent posting entries the client has not first approved is the firm determining entries, which ends the independence a review requires, so the owner's monthly acceptance rather than the firm's reviewer is the control the lender's report rests on.
  • C46. For transaction-level close work the external auditor will answer the client's agent with its own agent run over the whole population rather than rely on the client's, because standard setters warn that an AI's logic can change so that re-performance may not be possible and that auditors should not over-rely on the entity's AI, while the large firms already run agents over every journal line; so the client's agent earns no audit saving outside the frozen rules C26 describes, and its exceptions and the auditor's become two lists the controller must reconcile in fieldwork.
  • C49. When the auditor tests a client's agent-prepared entries with its own agent, as C46 expects, the two agents agreeing will not count as evidence, because language models' errors are correlated across providers as well as within them, so the auditor will sample entries on which both agents agree rather than only those they dispute, and the model behind the client's agent becomes a fact the auditor asks for at planning and again after each provider retirement C38 describes.
  • C50. In France the issuing of a sales invoice joins payment release and period lock as a point where an agent's error stops being cheap to undo, because from September 2026 for large and mid-size companies and September 2027 for smaller ones an invoice issued through an approved platform cannot be modified and can be corrected only by a new numbered invoice or a credit note sent the same way, so receivables agents there will draft invoices that a person validates before the platform, rather than post and be corrected before lock as C10 expects.
  • C51. The blind sample that measures a close agent's error rate will be drawn from the items the agent marks confident, because accountants already override the agent where it signals low confidence and follow it where it does not, so the errors that survive review sit among confident items, and a sample spread evenly or drawn from flagged exceptions measures the part people already catch.
  • C55. In Mexico the first binding exchange between one company's systems and another's in the close is already running, because a supplier's request to cancel an issued invoice must be accepted or rejected by the buyer within three business days, by a response signed with the buyer's own digital seal and sent through a web service, and silence counts as acceptance, so a buyer's agent that stops and waits for a reviewer, as C32 describes close agents doing, accepts every cancellation by default.
  • C56. Outside the Indian scheme of C41, a lender that can read the borrower's books directly will add that access to the audit rather than accept it in place of one, because what the audit buys a lender is an outside check of the accruals, which a live login shows but does not verify, so agent-prepared books will not by themselves move the loan size at which a bank requires an audit.
