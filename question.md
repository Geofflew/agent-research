# Research question

How will agentic product experiences in ERP and financial close platforms evolve over the next ten years: what work agents take on, how trust in that work is built and verified, how the products and their data have to be structured to support it, and what this does to the roles, the buyers, and the economics of the close?

## Sub-questions

- **work** — Which close tasks move to agents, in what order, and what decides the order (procedure clarity, data access, materiality, or risk)?
- **trust** — How does verified agent work get accepted by reviewers, auditors, and outside parties, and what evidence, controls, and error-rate history does each party need?
- **structure** — What does the platform have to hold as state (tasks, ownership, dependencies, procedures, policies, evidence) for agents to operate, and how does this change the ERP, the close tool, and the boundary between them?
- **roles-and-economics** — How do preparer, reviewer, and controller roles change, who the buyer becomes at each stage, and what happens to headcount, audit cost, and cost of capital?
