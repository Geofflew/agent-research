# Second reader vs autonomous reader

Definition: Three placements of automation in a review chain: automation as a concurrent aid to one human (CAD), automation replacing one of two human readers with risk-based escalation (MASAI), and automation holding the seat alone on a narrow pre-validated task (IDx-DR).

Sub-question: roles-and-economics

Related concepts: [[adoption-ladder]], [[preparer-and-reviewer-roles]], [[validation-harness]]

Evidence:
- Concurrent aid (CAD) showed no accuracy gain at population scale [lehman-cad-mammography-jama-im].
- AI replacing one of two readers in a randomised trial cut reading workload about 44% with detection at least as good [masai-trial-ai-mammography-screening].
- Regulator-cleared autonomy came first for a narrow task with prospective accuracy data and a referral path [fda-idx-dr-autonomous-ai-denovo].

- Disanalogy (C4, 0005A): MASAI safety rests on interval cancers counted within 2 years [mrc-masai-interval-cancer-endpoint]; close errors surface as restatements ~534 days of data, mostly in annual audit [mrc-audit-analytics-restatements-lag]. Close review seat is a tested control: threshold for investigation = precision, reviewer competence/authority, re-performance [mrc-pcaob-sapa11-review-precision], [mrc-pcaob-as2201-test-controls].

Open questions:
- Which close tasks resemble narrow screening (autonomous seat) vs judgment tasks (second reader)?
- What is the close equivalent of the double-reading rule: two agents, or agent plus one human, by risk tier?

Thesis: C20 — in the close, shortage removes the review seat before validation (CAD pattern, not MASAI); platform must produce validation on demand from own blind sample.
