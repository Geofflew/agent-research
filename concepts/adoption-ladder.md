# Adoption ladder

Definition: Four levels of agent adoption in the close, where each level produces the record the next one needs. Level 1 automation (agents work, people approve). Level 2 verification (trust is the result). Level 3 headless (agents act, no person in the loop). Level 4 external facing (outsiders act on the numbers).

Sub-question: roles-and-economics

Related concepts: [[validation-harness]], [[preparer-and-reviewer-roles]], [[agent-skills-over-state]]

Evidence:
- Seed concept from an illustrative architecture sketch; no sources yet. Level 1 builds a draft-and-approve loop with draft and final kept as records, produces hours saved and a record of each draft against its final, and the outcome is a faster close at the same headcount with the controller as buyer. Level 2 builds controls verifying the agent did what it says on every run, produces AI work verified without reading it and a known error rate, and the outcome is auditor reliance and lower audit cost with the audit committee as buyer. Level 3 builds write authority under policy, per decision class, revocable, produces a close that runs itself with people handling exceptions, and the outcome is a two-day close with roles not backfilled and the CFO as buyer. Level 4 builds identity for outside parties and a shared record schema, produces outsiders verifying a figure without asking the company, and the outcome is cheaper capital, a standing audit, and machine settlement. The hand-offs are: level 1's draft-versus-final history becomes level 2's error rate; level 2's verified record licenses level 3; level 3's record of autonomous work is what level 4 exposes.
