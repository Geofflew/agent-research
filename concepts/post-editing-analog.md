# Post-editing analog (translation industry)

Definition: Machine translation produced the draft and the human translator became a post-editor; the pattern of volume, roles, standards, and pay that followed is the closest documented analog to level 1 of agent adoption in the close.

Sub-question: roles-and-economics

Related concepts: [[preparer-and-reviewer-roles]], [[automation-bias]], [[adoption-ladder]]

Evidence:
- DGT eTranslation: 19M pages 2017 to 890M 2025; DGT staff 1,895, 68% language experts; no staff cut attributed [mtpe-dgt-in-figures].
- MTPE share of LSP projects 26% (2022) to ~46% (2024) [mtpe-nimdzi-efficiency-gap].
- ISO 18587:2017 requires full, human post-editing of MT output and sets post-editor competences [mtpe-iso-18587-scope].
- Post-editing consistently faster than from scratch; gain varies by system and language [mtpe-divemt-arxiv].
- Practitioners advise hourly rates; per-word MTPE rates make careful review unaffordable [mtpe-ata-division-ata63-rates].
- Headcount did fall, via attrition and freelance demand: Canada Translation Bureau cutting 339 of ~1,350 positions by attrition 2025-30 [mtpe2-cbc-translation-bureau-cut]; US areas with higher Google Translate adoption lost translator employment [mtpe2-frey-llanos-paredes-voxeu]; 43% of UK translators report income drop [mtpe2-cnn-translators-losing-work]. Thesis C9 (0005A) reads this as: seat survives, unreplaced hires and per-task outside work go.
- Raw MT accepted by US agencies only with a disclaimer that only the English version is relied on in decisions and court [mtpe-routefifty-gov-machine-translation].

Open questions:
